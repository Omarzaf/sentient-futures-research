# India and Pakistan protein consumption research

The current research direction is a comparative brief on the social, cultural, and economic drivers of protein consumption and possible alternative-protein adoption in India and Pakistan. Policy is considered primarily as a driver of consumption and market development. A related contribution concerns the design and review of forecast-elicitation questions.

The consolidated brief for this scope is [Protein Consumption and Alternative Protein Adoption in India and Pakistan](research/india-pakistan/index.html), reviewed through 14 September 2026. It is a secondary synthesis and a proposed research design, not a completed comparative study, and its final human review is pending. The broader reports in this repository are earlier research inputs and should not be mistaken for an agreed final team output.

## Questions to investigate

The brief narrows these to a primary comparison of a plant-based chicken product against a conventional chicken option and a familiar pulse-based option in a specified meal, with cultivated meat and fermentation-derived proteins treated separately.

- How do affordability, food preferences, dietary rules, social status, and access interact for specific products and consumer groups?
- When does a substitute replace an animal-source food, supplement it, or compete with an existing plant-based meal?
- Which public policies, certification arrangements, and commercial institutions affect adoption?
- Which forecast questions have clear definitions, baselines, time horizons, and observable resolution sources?

Country-level labels are starting points for investigation. They do not establish uniform national dietary preferences. Claims about a lack of policy activity require a documented institutional and date-bounded search. The repository does not establish either claim.

## Evidence still needed

The comparative dataset contains an India supply observation but no Pakistan observation. Food supply is not individual dietary intake. A completed India–Pakistan comparison requires appropriate country sources, attention to differences within countries, and compatible measurement definitions. No missing values have been imputed to complete the comparison.

The brief adds national household-survey evidence for both countries, but deliberately does not place those statistics on one intake scale. What it still lacks is primary evidence: no interviews, tasting trials, consumer survey, or forecasting experiment were conducted, and no verified national alternative-protein sales figures were established. Its claim that Pakistan has no directly comparable smart-protein programme is bounded to the sources reviewed and does not establish an absence of policy.

This scope note is a shareable adaptation of the current project plan. It excludes personal preparation, meeting logistics, and private document links. It does not claim mentor approval or agreement on behalf of other participants.
