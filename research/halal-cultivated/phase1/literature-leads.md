# Literature leads for gaps G2 and G7

Found 1 October 2026 with Consensus searches (titles and abstracts only; no full text read). These are secondary sources. They do not close either gap, change any claim or school-question row, or count as a named-authority ruling. Each needs reading in full and checking against the primary fatwa or text it relies on.

## G2: ruling on the cultivated-cell process

What the abstracts say, by theme. Authors summarise the same few fatwas and the same classical analogies, so agreement between them is not independent confirmation.

- **Cell source decides the status.** Cells from an animal slaughtered by Islamic rules are described as permissible. Cells from a living animal, from a non-halal animal, or from impure material are described as not. Hamdan et al. 2019 (Journal of Fatwa Management and Research) states this most directly. Hamdan et al. 2018 (Journal of Religion and Health) adds that no blood or serum may be used. Qudsiyah et al. 2024 reaches the same conclusion by a systematic literature review.
- **Six-principle test.** Kashim et al. 2022 (Saudi Journal of Biological Sciences) frames halal status through halal and haram animals, slaughter, freedom from najs, istihalah tammah, maslahah against mafsadah, and darurat. It names embryonic and myosatellite stem cells and the animal-blood culture medium as disputed points.
- **Stage-by-stage mapping.** Alqurashi et al. 2026 (Foods) maps each production stage onto halal requirements and lists the same open points: starter-cell origin, slaughter of the donor, biopsied tissue, and fetal bovine serum. It concludes that unresolved interpretations and regulatory fragmentation limit adoption.
- **No definitive ruling.** Mechenene et al. 2025 (Journal of Posthumanism) says scholarly bodies have given conditions but no final ruling. Hamdan et al. 2024 (Heliyon) reviews the fatwa and scholar discourse and recommends that the International Islamic Fiqh Academy deliberate. This agrees with the repo's existing position that no named authority has ruled on the exact process.

Possible next step for G2: read the Hamdan 2024 review and the Alqurashi 2026 article in full and list which named bodies and fatwas they cite, then locate each in the original. Check the claim attributed to the Hamdan 2019 and Rahmawati 2023 papers that living-animal cells are not halal against the Phase One treatment of the live-donor reading, which already withdrew an earlier IIFA citation.

## G7: istihalah and gelatin

- **Two positions on gelatin.** Akbar et al. 2020 (Journal of Islamic and Religious Studies, Urdu) reports that the 3rd Fiqh Conference in Makkah did not permit gelatin from pig tissue, while the Islamic Fiqh Academy of India held that gelatin is permissible through istihalah, relying on al-Shaybani as cited in Radd al-Muhtar. Tukiran et al. 2023 and Jamaludin et al. 2011 argue that gelatin's chemical composition stays intact, so pig-derived gelatin stays haram absent necessity.
- **Process depth matters.** Simsek 2019 and Gultekin et al. 2020 (both Turkish) say proteins undergo istihalah only if fully broken down to amino acids. They say gelatin is not accepted as transformed, and that pig material is excluded whatever the process. This supports G7's caution against treating English chemical-change language as automatic purification.
- **Contrary applied view.** Imron et al. 2025 and Gunay et al. 2023, on vaccines, argue that pig-derived gelatin can change status through istihalah and that trypsin is removed by purification.
- **Contested at school level.** Rahim et al. 2023 says jurists and schools differ on istihalah. Miswanto et al. 2023 (Az-Zarqa') classifies istihalah into physical, chemical and physical-chemical models from the texts. It lists the liver and spleen of halal animals under the last. Check this against G-M-LIVER and G6 before reuse, since the repo holds that the Maliki liver and spleen locator is unverified.

Possible next step for G7: fetal bovine serum and enzymes in cell-culture media were not searched in this pass because of rate limits. The vaccine literature on trypsin is the closest analogue found.
