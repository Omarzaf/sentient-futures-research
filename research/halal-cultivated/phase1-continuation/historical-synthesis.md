# What the historical parallels can support

1 October 2026. The 21 cases cover distinct evidence types. The following mechanisms are analytical inferences for research design; no row predicts a date or quantitative effect. Human review is pending.

## religious ruling

A named authority can classify a defined substance or impose process controls; the examples preserve different views and authority scopes. A familiar legal category plus complete process evidence could make a bounded decision easier to formulate.

Counterpoint: IIFA gelatin/stunning deferrals and disputed transformation examples show that familiarity need not settle facts or consensus.

Limit: No ruling deadline, universal permission or market response is estimated. Purity, ingestion, necessity and product certification are separate.

## price

Availability histories and a product-choice model distinguish categories and relative prices. If eligibility, supply and format are already established, a price change is a purchase-response hypothesis to test; its timing relative to production expansion is not established here.

Counterpoint: Butter/margarine reversals and simultaneous growth in Pakistan categories undermine one-direction displacement stories.

Limit: No observed price/quantity ratio is treated as an identified elasticity. Purchase response cannot by itself settle a religious ruling.

Cases: [HP-14](historical-cases.md#hp-14), [HP-15](historical-cases.md#hp-15), [HP-16](historical-cases.md#hp-16), [HP-17](historical-cases.md#hp-17). Claim IDs are recorded in the [structured synthesis](historical-synthesis.json).

## scarcity

Supply shortfalls can motivate alternative sources, output expansion or changed product use. Existing suppliers and known products offer a candidate response channel; activation time and persistence remain case-specific and unmeasured for cultivated meat.

Counterpoint: Recovery of incumbent supplies can reverse the apparent transition; forecasts and announcements can fail to become output.

Limit: Qatar company milestones and China output are distinct units; scarcity alone does not establish legal necessity for every product.

Cases: [HP-16](historical-cases.md#hp-16), [HP-18](historical-cases.md#hp-18), [HP-19](historical-cases.md#hp-19), [HP-20](historical-cases.md#hp-20). Claim IDs are recorded in the [structured synthesis](historical-synthesis.json).

## disease

Disease and control measures remove conventional supply; published series also show recovery/rebound. A supply shock can make substitute supply relevant during the interruption.

Counterpoint: The same event may alter trade, prices and behavior; it need not generate durable alternative-protein adoption.

Limit: No reduced contamination risk for cultivated products is inferred, and output/price changes do not identify a demand coefficient.

Cases: [HP-18](historical-cases.md#hp-18), [HP-19](historical-cases.md#hp-19). Claim IDs are recorded in the [structured synthesis](historical-synthesis.json).

## technology

Engineered ingredients, mechanised slaughter, management controls and production expansion raise different technical questions. A documented compliant substitute input or established production method may narrow an authority’s review question.

Counterpoint: Technical change can create new provenance questions; Baladna planning predates the blockade and does not measure greenfield build time.

Limit: Physical throughput, validated input identity, certification and consumer acceptance remain distinct; no AI acceleration estimate is supplied.

Cases: [HP-05](historical-cases.md#hp-05), [HP-07](historical-cases.md#hp-07), [HP-09](historical-cases.md#hp-09), [HP-15](historical-cases.md#hp-15), [HP-17](historical-cases.md#hp-17), [HP-20](historical-cases.md#hp-20). Claim IDs are recorded in the [structured synthesis](historical-synthesis.json).

## policy or sovereignty

Local prohibitions, quality-control institutions and food-security strategies describe governmental or organisational action. Defined mandates, prior investment and functioning institutions can provide a route for a specific decision or supply response.

Counterpoint: Strategy targets are not outcomes; emergency action for conventional dairy does not imply immediate cultivated-product approval.

Limit: The UAE example is not a six-country GCC evaluation. Legal access, religious status and national supply shares must each be evidenced.

Cases: [HP-13](historical-cases.md#hp-13), [HP-16](historical-cases.md#hp-16), [HP-20](historical-cases.md#hp-20), [HP-21](historical-cases.md#hp-21). Claim IDs are recorded in the [structured synthesis](historical-synthesis.json).

## taste

The sources identify product differences, resemblance disputes and changing choices; they do not isolate a sensory-treatment effect. HP-12 supplies religious/species context only, not sensory or cuisine-fit evidence. A familiar sensory role could be tested as a condition for substitution within a dish or purchasing occasion.

Counterpoint: Resemblance can raise adulteration/trust concerns; demand-model differentiation is not a direct taste experiment.

Limit: All causal taste and cuisine coefficients remain unknown. No national preference or quick-ruling effect is inferred.

Cases: [HP-12](historical-cases.md#hp-12), [HP-14](historical-cases.md#hp-14), [HP-15](historical-cases.md#hp-15), [HP-16](historical-cases.md#hp-16). Claim IDs are recorded in the [structured synthesis](historical-synthesis.json).

The useful precedent is conditional: a defined process, named decision-maker and functioning implementation route can narrow a question. None of these analogues establishes a cultivated-meat approval date, uptake rate, universal religious gate or compatible demand baseline.
