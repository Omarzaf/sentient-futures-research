# Halal standards: what the available editions establish

1 October 2026. Two agent readings independently reconciled; human review pending. This is a research comparison, not certification advice.

The two standards are identifiable, but their full operative requirements have not been read. The public evidence supports edition and access distinctions, Pakistan's modified-adoption metadata, and a scoped UAE administrative reference. It does not determine a cultivated product's compliance.

| Instrument | Evidence read | Remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OIC/SMIIC 1:2019 | Publisher overview, catalogue and project metadata | Full normative clauses unavailable in inspected publisher pages; offered for purchase |
| PS 3733-2022 (R) | PSQCA calls it a modified adoption of the second edition of OIC/SMIIC 1:2019 | Modifications, operative clauses and enforceability not established by the list |
| GSO 2055-1:2015 | Official store labels edition 2 current; linked English preview, printed pages iv and 1–2, read visually | Preview ends at definition 3.9; sections 4–8 and Annex 1 are absent |
| GSO TC15/DS1 2055-1:2026 | Official project page labels an initial Arabic draft prepared by SASO | No adopted replacement or national effective date established |
| UAE.S 2055-1 | MOIAT's indexed official page lists it within the UAE halal system | Edition absent; full Cabinet 10/2014 instrument and cultivated-product scope unread |

The GSO preview separates references for slaughter, certification bodies, accreditation, hygiene and labelling. Its definitions are not a donor-procurement or serum ruling. Its foreword makes Arabic controlling in a conflict and says the document replaces the 2009 version. The store's English preview link was checked in original HTML, avoiding a search-returned test-host version with different metadata.

Pakistan's modified adoption must be read as its own national text. The UAE page explains a certification scheme and an optional National Mark; it is not evidence that every food requires that mark. Regional membership and publisher participation do not establish automatic national applicability. Existing Saudi, Indian and other country-route evidence remains separate.
