## Findings that affect interpretation

**Iraq's observations are not interchangeable.** Pew's later account reports late-2011 survey self-identification: 51% Shia, 42% Sunni and 5% just Muslim. Sampling details are not reconstructed here. Subtracting this from the 2009 synthesized estimate would not measure demographic change. The unreported remainder stays unallocated. [Pew's 2014 account](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2014/06/18/the-sunni-shia-divide-where-they-live-what-they-believe-and-how-they-view-each-other/).

**Personal-status law has a limited subject.** Iraq's code-approval decision was signed on 30 September 2025 and published on 6 October 2025; publication is its commencement trigger. The Scientific Council's drafting/advisory role does not establish food-fatwa competence. The judiciary's March article predates approval and is commentary, not legislation. [Enabling law](https://iraqld.msla.iq/legislations/showlegislation?sc=190220253585917), [approval decision](https://iraqld.msla.iq/legislations/showlegislation?lawbookid=53936), [earlier commentary](https://www.sjc.iq/view.76245/).

**Document identity constrains legal conclusions.** Iran's Article 12 qualifies local-school rules and protects other schools; the two hosts reproduce one constitutional lineage. Yemen's compilation appends a February 2015 transitional declaration and differs from the separate federal draft. Qatar's passage is explanatory; Kuwait's Article 2 names no school in that clause. None of these limited readings establishes a complete current legal order or proves that no other law names a school. [Second Iran transcription](https://fis-iran.org/document/constitution-of-the-islamic-republic-of-iran/), [Yemen metadata](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/19992).
