# Six factors mapped to the shared forecast drivers

1 October 2026. This is a plan-derived vocabulary and a set of testable hypotheses, not an estimated adoption model. All weights and coefficients remain unknown; the compatible demand baseline and external workstream acceptance are unresolved.

| Mind-map factor | Shared driver names | Operational definition |
|---|---|---|
| Mass-scaling production | supply chain costs; AI-driven R&D speed | Physical throughput, input/logistics cost, and time to a validated production process are separate mechanisms. |
| Cheap | price | Consumer price relative to a defined conventional comparator, measured in the same unit, channel, place, date and price basis. |
| Taste | taste and texture | Sensory performance for a specified product format, preparation and comparison task. |
| Premade | convenience | Preparation time, storage, handling and availability in a specified retail or foodservice use. |
| Nutrition | health perceptions | Beliefs about nutrition and health, measured separately from laboratory composition, safety and clinical outcomes. |
| Variety and cuisine | cuisine fit | Ability of a named product to perform the desired role in a locally specified dish and cooking method. |

## DR-01 — Mass-scaling production

Supply expansion may reduce cost or improve availability; AI may shorten a particular research step. A lower laboratory cost may not survive scale-up; faster research may leave validation, approval and capital constraints unchanged.

Socio-cultural question: Trusted halal inputs, donor provenance and certification capacity can constrain which scale-up route is usable.

Possible variable paths: K, p_mass. Do not reuse one scale assumption as duplicate adjustments. Separate any documented capacity change from a separately evidenced price-mediated purchase response, and record baseline mechanisms before transfer. No AI acceleration coefficient is observed in these cases.

Historical pointers: [HP-05](historical-cases.md#hp-05), [HP-07](historical-cases.md#hp-07), [HP-09](historical-cases.md#hp-09), [HP-17](historical-cases.md#hp-17), [HP-20](historical-cases.md#hp-20).

## DR-02 — Cheap

A lower relative price may increase purchase within an already eligible product market. Margarine’s reversal despite a price advantage cautions that low price alone does not secure durable demand.

Socio-cultural question: Affordability interacts with household resources, trust and whether a lower-price substitute fits a valued dish.

Possible variable paths: p_mass. Historical nominal product prices and equilibrium displacement ratios are not transferable elasticities. Use the active bridge price conversion only with compatible inputs.

Historical pointers: [HP-14](historical-cases.md#hp-14), [HP-15](historical-cases.md#hp-15), [HP-16](historical-cases.md#hp-16), [HP-18](historical-cases.md#hp-18), [HP-19](historical-cases.md#hp-19).

## DR-03 — Taste

A product that meets sensory expectations may increase trial and repeat purchase. A familiar format may fail in a different cooking technique; sensory trials may not predict repeated buying at market prices.

Socio-cultural question: Meal roles and family expectations are testable moderators, not fixed national traits.

Possible variable paths: p_mass. No controlled sensory effect or country coefficient is estimated by this history review. Do not relabel product differentiation as a measured taste effect.

Historical pointers: [HP-14](historical-cases.md#hp-14), [HP-15](historical-cases.md#hp-15), [HP-16](historical-cases.md#hp-16).

## DR-04 — Premade

A suitable ready-made format may reduce practical costs for a particular purchasing group. Cold-chain, equipment or limited distribution can erase convenience gains; convenience may be secondary to price or religious eligibility.

Socio-cultural question: Who purchases and prepares the meal, shared eating and retail access may change the mechanism.

Possible variable paths: p_mass, K. Keep format demand separate from distribution capacity. HP-19 and HP-20 are formulation, distribution and capacity constraints only, not measured Premade effects. The historical cases provide mechanisms to investigate, not an estimated convenience effect.

Historical pointers: [HP-19](historical-cases.md#hp-19), [HP-20](historical-cases.md#hp-20).

## DR-05 — Nutrition

Perceived health advantages may influence product choice. Beliefs can reverse or diverge from evidence; product reformulation and public advice can change comparisons.

Socio-cultural question: Trusted sources, ingredient familiarity and understandings of naturalness may mediate beliefs.

Possible variable paths: p_mass. No nutritional equivalence, safety benefit or clinical conclusion is inferred. Medical necessity precedents do not establish ordinary-food demand.

Historical pointers: [HP-06](historical-cases.md#hp-06), [HP-08](historical-cases.md#hp-08), [HP-14](historical-cases.md#hp-14), [HP-19](historical-cases.md#hp-19).

## DR-06 — Variety and cuisine

Performance in familiar dishes may support repeat purchase and substitution in that use. Success in a nugget or beverage does not establish performance in a whole-cut, stewed or high-heat preparation.

Socio-cultural question: Compare within-market dishes and consumer segments; avoid assigning a single cuisine preference to an entire country.

Possible variable paths: p_mass. HP-12 is religious/species context only, not sensory or cuisine-fit evidence. Cuisine fit is the plan’s proposed shared field. Its external workstream adoption and numerical effects have not been verified.

Historical pointers: [HP-12](historical-cases.md#hp-12), [HP-15](historical-cases.md#hp-15), [HP-16](historical-cases.md#hp-16).
