# Cultivated chicken in India, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and the UAE: where the evidence stands

2 October 2026. Draft for mentor review. AI-assisted; human verification pending, and no claim has had human scholarly review. This page summarises the [October continuation](README.md) of the [30 September Phase One review](../phase1/review.md). The country-by-country detail is in the [gate table](gate-table.md).

## The short answer

No focal country has approved a cultivated chicken product. India is the closest on the regulatory side and the least documented on the religious side. Among the religious authorities retained here, the one process all of them would accept in principle has not been made. The one class of product that actually exists has not been ruled on by any of them.

- Saudi Arabia: A food-authorization route exists. SFDA's novel-food regulation covers animal cell culture, and halal sourcing is built into approval for animal-origin food, so the religious and legal gates move together across the whole market. No approval was found. The SFDA co-organised the September 2024 Jeddah seminar that fed IIFA's Resolution 265, which makes IIFA's text the most directly relevant religious instrument for Saudi Arabia. Three senior Saudi scholars advising GOOD Meat in 2023 required a slaughtered donor; IIFA's final text keeps a living-donor route as well.
- United Arab Emirates: This is the weakest case on evidence. A slaughtered-donor position by the UAE Council for Fatwa is reported only by a tertiary source, with no instrument, number or date located. Abu Dhabi announced a single-window novel-food framework in October 2025 covering registration, halal certification and import permits. Whether it now operates is unknown.
- Pakistan: No food-authorization route for cultivated meat was identified. Punjab's generic product registration is the nearest documented procedure. A newspaper reports that scholars led by Mufti Muhammad Taqi Usmani require a slaughtered donor; the original ruling was not located. A 2019 fatwa from Banuri Town in Karachi, in Urdu and held for translation, sets ingredient and source conditions and withholds a final ruling.
- India: FSSAI's September 2026 register lists Biokraft Foods' cultivated chicken application as under process at a stage that covers clarification, approval or rejection. No ruling from Darul Uloom Deoband, the Islamic Fiqh Academy India or a Barelvi institution was located. Non-halal chicken is legal, and Uttar Pradesh restricted halal-certified food in 2023, so the halal gate covers a segment of buyers rather than the market. That segment's size is unmeasured.

## The question that decides the religious gate

Every retained authority agrees on two things. The source animal must be one Muslims may eat, and no forbidden input may enter the culture. They part on where the founder cells come from.

| Founder cells | Who accepts | Who rejects |
|---|---|---|
| Taken from a bird slaughtered by Islamic rules | IIFA; the Saudi advisers to GOOD Meat; MUIS; the reported UAE and Pakistani positions; Wifaq ul Ulama (UK); the American Halal Foundation | No retained authority |
| Biopsy from a living bird | IIFA Resolution 265, on its English and Arabic text | Saudi advisers to GOOD Meat; Nahdlatul Ulama (2021); American Fiqh Academy; American Halal Foundation; the reported UAE and Pakistani positions |
| Derived from an egg or embryo | Argued for by Hamdan and colleagues (2024); MUIS's booklet mentions an egg or embryo exception that it does not reconcile with its slaughter wording | Not addressed directly by any focal-country authority |
| Reprogrammed from feathers | American Fiqh Academy | Not addressed by any focal-country authority |

The third row matters most, and it is the least settled. GOOD Meat's line reviewed by the US FDA derives from 10-day chicken embryos. Vital Meat's 2025 Singapore approval uses embryonic stem cells. These are the cultivated chicken products that exist. They fall outside the slaughtered and living-donor categories that the rulings discuss. GOOD Meat itself said in September 2023 that its approved cell line did not meet its Saudi advisers' guidance. Its US process also used fetal bovine serum, which several of these authorities exclude.

One more complication sits inside IIFA. A Kuwaiti news post from October 2024 describes the Jeddah seminar outcome as requiring a slaughtered donor. The final resolution, adopted in May 2025, keeps a living-donor alternative. If both are accurate, part of the apparent dispute is IIFA's own drafting history. Press coverage of the resolution also tends to report only the slaughter condition.

## What the evidence can carry

- The continuation register holds 174 sources and 151 claims. Of these, 129 claims had a second AI read of the same retrieved source, 17 are 2 October search leads whose originals could not be opened, and 5 are labelled inferences.
- No claim has had human review. "Independently checked" tests transcription and reading, not whether the right source was found.
- Network restrictions in the working environment blocked the IIFA, FDA, USDA, NBS and Pew sites on 2 October. Every 2 October lead needs confirming against its original.
- Urdu, Malay and Indonesian texts are held under the English-first rule, including the Banuri Town fatwa and Malaysia's 2025 national fatwa council opinion.

## The second-order work, in one line each

- Demand elasticities: The register holds 186 published estimates for chicken, beef and mutton in the four countries. None can serve as a forecast parameter. Several tables violate basic demand theory, the categories do not match, and none measures substitution between cultivated and conventional chicken. If price sensitivity is needed, take it from the baseline model behind the OECD-FAO projections, or from meta-analyses as stated priors.
- Insects as poultry feed: No national market value or chicken-cost effect was established in any country. IIFA postponed its ruling on insects in May 2025 (Resolution 267). The next useful question is whether insect meal is a legal poultry-feed ingredient in each country.
- Historical parallels: The 21 cases show mechanisms, not speeds. The closest parallels are animal-derived inputs replaced by engineered ones: IIFA 210 expressly permits genetically engineered rennet, and recombinant insulin replaced porcine insulin. These can answer the timing question if their dates are assembled.
- Muslim geography: Pew's 2009 Shia estimates and constitutional texts describe context. They do not set any gate, and juristic-school shares remain unmeasured.

## Questions for mentors

1. How should a cell line derived from an egg or embryo be classified? This decides the halal status of every cultivated chicken product that exists today. Who is qualified to answer it for Hanafi and Hanbali audiences?
2. Should the research prioritise the Saudi route, which has law and an engaged regulator, or the Indian route, which has a named pending application?
3. Is a halal-requiring segment in India worth sizing now, given that the religious layer there has no institutional text yet?
