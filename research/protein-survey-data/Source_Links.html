S01 · Chriki et al. (2024)

Attitudes toward artificial meat in Arab countries

Arab countries (pooled). Sample: 1,025.

Primary source · Read or download source · DOI

Abstract verified; full-text access not established. These source documents are linked, not included as local files.

Data access: No public respondent-level file verified.

Pooled attitudes, not national adoption. Chat used ~41% willing to try; abstract says >40%. Correct DOI is 1750-3841, not the earlier 1754-3841.

S02 · Maqsood et al. (2025)

Are Emirati consumers in United Arab Emirates open to alternative proteins?

UAE nationals, ages 18–65. Sample: 1,666.

Primary source · Read or download source · DOI

Open full text and supplementary-material page. These source documents are linked, not included as local files.

Data access: Article/supplements; further inquiries to corresponding author.

Preferences, not consumption. Women and ages 18–24 dominate sample; excludes expatriates.

S03 · Dean et al. (2023)

Should I Really Pay a Premium for This?

12 countries; includes Pakistan and Indonesia. Sample: 4,488.

Primary source · Read or download source · DOI

Public university-repository PDF. These source documents are linked, not included as local files.

Data access: Available from corresponding author upon request.

Online publication 2023; journal issue 2024. Pakistan/Indonesia sample counts are not consumption rates.

S04 · Alptekin et al. (2026)

Consumer acceptance of cultured meat and its determinants among Turkish adults

Türkiye, adults. Sample: 1,169.

Primary source · Read or download source · DOI

Supplementary PDF 1

Supplementary PDF 2

Abstract public; full article subscription-limited; supplementary PDFs public. These source documents are linked, not included as local files.

Data access: No public respondent-level file verified.

Willingness to try, not consumption. Perceived unnaturalness, safety concerns and distrust were barriers.

S05 · Alabdulmohsen and Al-Otaibi (2025)

Exploring Novel Foods: Edible Insect Acceptance in Saudi Arabia

Saudi Arabia, adults. Sample: 2,208.

Primary source · Read or download source · DOI

Open full text. These source documents are linked, not included as local files.

Data access: Published contributions in article; inquiries to corresponding author.

Prior insect consumption by sex. Not evidence about plant-based or cultivated meat. Non-probability recruitment limits generalisation.

S06 · Terano et al. (2023)

Exploring Muslim Consumers’ Acceptance of Cultured Beef Meat

Muslims in urban Selangor, Malaysia. Sample: 102.

Primary source · Read or download source · DOI

Open publisher PDF. These source documents are linked, not included as local files.

Data access: No public respondent-level file verified.

Abstract reports 44.1% acceptance; Table 3 reports 50/102 (49.0%). Table 1 uses 44.1% for awareness. Retain discrepancy; not a national estimate.

S07 · Rakuten Insight (2025)

Plant-based food alternatives, June 2025

12 Asian markets; six Southeast Asian countries extracted. Sample: 78,816.

Primary source · Read or download source

Public report PDF. These source documents are linked, not included as local files.

Data access: No public respondent-level file verified.

Country samples do not isolate Muslims. National representativeness not established. Broad plant-food measures include milk; do not treat them as meat consumption.

S08 · GFI APAC / The Good Growth Co. (2024)

Indonesia; Malaysia; Philippines; Singapore; Thailand; Vietnam. Sample: 5,971.

Primary source · Read or download source

Public report and publisher summary. These source documents are linked, not included as local files.

S09 · Lwin, Malik, Ho and Schulz (2026)

Feeding the future

Singapore. Sample: 2,000.

Primary source · Read or download source · DOI

University publication record and abstract verified. These source documents are linked, not included as local files.

Data access: No public respondent-level file verified.

Journal issue March 2026; first online 2025. Acceptance comparison, not consumption prevalence.

Values describe different populations and measures. Willingness, preferences, awareness and reported past consumption should be analysed separately.
