Alt Protein and Food Systems Orientation

Technology, capital, talent, regulation, foresight practice, and prior art

Status: Earlier orientation draft; evidence cutoff 5 September 2026. AI-assisted; human verification pending. This is background reading, not the current project scope or a current market update.

BOUNDARY This is an orientation and evidence map. It does not select a thesis, mapping method, scenario architecture, or final deliverable.


How to read this briefing

The document separates four things that are often collapsed: commercial product availability, technical feasibility, regulatory permission, and industrial-scale economics. A technology can clear one threshold while remaining far from the others. It also treats source incentives as part of the evidence. GFI is a sector-building advocacy nonprofit; company announcements are claims by interested parties; investor databases omit undisclosed transactions; and commissioned techno-economic analyses depend heavily on sponsor-supplied assumptions. (GFI investment methodology, advocacy, 2026; CE Delft, GFI-commissioned analysis, 2021)

The reports titled “2026 State of the Industry” describe calendar-year 2025 activity. GFI changed its naming convention in 2026 so the title matches publication year; the observation period did not change. Where this briefing says “2026,” it means an event that occurred during 2026, not a report published in 2026 about 2025. (GFI State of Global Policy, advocacy, Apr. 2026, pp. 2-3)

Source labels used here are: official for statutes, regulators, and programme owners; peer-reviewed for academic journals; original analysis for a research group’s own synthesis or interviews; advocacy for mission-driven field builders; philanthropic for funder statements; trade or professional for sector press; and company for issuer claims. Dates and page numbers appear inline where available. “Inference” marks this briefing’s synthesis rather than a source’s explicit conclusion.

Executive orientation

BOTTOM LINE Plant-based foods remain the only track with a measurable mass retail market. Fermentation has commercial ingredients and meaningful infrastructure activity, but little transparent category revenue. Cultivated meat has accumulated approvals and demonstrations, not industrial-scale economics. The capital contraction is real, but the headline figures are frequently misdated or overgeneralized.

Global retail sales of plant-based meat, seafood, milk, yogurt, ice cream, cheese, and eggs reached $28.9 billion in 2025, up 3 percent in current nominal dollars. Plant-based meat and seafood represented $6.6 billion. These data are Euromonitor estimates reproduced by GFI, so they are modeled retail-market data rather than audited producer revenue. (GFI Plant-Based State of Industry, advocacy using Euromonitor, Apr. 2026, p. 17)

Private investment across GFI’s alternative-protein company universe fell from $1.6 billion in 2023 to $1.1 billion in 2024 and $881 million in 2025. The statement “investment fell below $1 billion in 2026” is false as a calendar-year claim: $881 million is a 2025 total published in 2026. The 2026 year is incomplete as of this briefing. (GFI investment reports and methodology, advocacy using Net Zero Insights, 2024-2026)

The “70+ companies” claim does not come from GFI. Green Queen’s rolling tracker counted more than 70 companies that had been acquired, merged, become insolvent, or ceased operations since September 2024. Those outcomes are analytically different, and the tracker’s public article does not provide a stable, complete dataset or reproducible inclusion rule. GFI separately identified at least 19 plant-based acquisitions in 2025, plus an unspecified number of pauses and closures. (Green Queen, trade press, 16 Jun. 2026; GFI Plant-Based report, advocacy, Apr. 2026, pp. 10-12)

Europe’s reported 56 percent funding increase is real only in a narrow sense: private company investment rose year on year to €236 million in the first half of 2026, and fermentation captured €199 million, or 84 percent. Deal count fell by roughly half. Separately, European public research and innovation funding fell in 2025 for the first time in GFI Europe’s series. The “European funding rebound” therefore does not describe the whole capital system. (GFI Europe, advocacy using Net Zero Insights, 27 Aug. 2026; GFI Europe R&I tracker, advocacy, 2026)

The public evidence does not support a present-tense claim that artificial intelligence has moved the sector’s binding constraint from capital to computational talent. A June 2026 Rethink Priorities study found sparse realized adoption, acute data-sharing constraints, limited sector-specific validation, and persistent scale-up infrastructure problems. It also notes that conventional animal agriculture has larger datasets and capital pools and could capture similar AI gains. (Rethink Priorities, original analysis based on literature and five expert interviews, 9 Jun. 2026)

Foresight is well established in food systems, but its center of gravity is participatory systems work and quantitative land-use modeling, not technology-transition monitoring. The evidence base can support driver mapping, structured scenario logics, backcasting, Delphi, morphological analysis, and cross-impact balance. It is weaker for calibrated probability estimates and generic leading indicators because private firms do not disclose comparable yields, capacity utilization, unit costs, or failed batches. (Foresight4Food and UN Food Systems Coordination Hub, practitioner guide, Jul. 2025; Pedde et al., peer-reviewed review, 2025; inference)

Appendix: claim audit and terminology


1. Technology and market

Alternative protein is a market umbrella, not one technology. Four tracks matter for this assignment: plant-based formulation and structuring; biomass fermentation, in which microbial biomass is the product; precision fermentation, in which engineered organisms make a target protein or fat; and cultivated meat, in which animal cells are grown outside an animal. Hybrid products combine tracks. Traditional fermentation, algae, insects, and molecular farming are adjacent categories, but GFI’s investment and market series do not include all of them consistently. (GFI State of Industry reports, advocacy, Apr. 2026)

Plant-based sales are geographically broad but uneven. In 2025, Europe accounted for $10.1 billion across the broad category, Asia-Pacific $9.2 billion, North America $6.9 billion, Latin America $1.7 billion, and the Middle East and Africa $1.1 billion. Meat and seafood were a much smaller share in Asia-Pacific than dairy-adjacent categories. Current-dollar growth therefore should not be read as unit growth, and category totals should not be read as “plant-based meat.” (GFI Plant-Based report, advocacy using Euromonitor, Apr. 2026, p. 17)

Illustrative active firms differ by track. Plant-based includes Beyond Meat, Impossible Foods, Heura, and the Kraft Heinz NotCo joint venture. Precision fermentation includes Perfect Day, The EVERY Company, Remilk, Imagindairy, Vivici, Onego Bio, Formo, and Verley. Biomass fermentation includes Marlow Foods/Quorn, MyForest Foods, Prime Roots, Nature’s Fynd, and The Protein Brewery. Cultivated products with regulatory milestones include UPSIDE Foods, GOOD Meat, Mission Barns, Wildtype, Vow, PARIMA, and Aleph Farms. This list signals operating and regulatory relevance; it is not a market-share ranking. (GFI company databases and 2026 reports, advocacy, Apr. 2026; FDA inventory, official, updated 19 Feb. 2026; Singapore SFA approved-novel-food list, official, 14 Aug. 2026)

The 2025 capital contraction coincided with consolidation. GFI counted 142 specialist cultivated-meat companies at year end, down from 155 in 2024, and 163 specialist fermentation companies. Meatable entered dissolution after failing to secure financing; Believer Meats ceased operations after building a large North Carolina facility and receiving an FDA consultation outcome. These cases support a multi-causal account: financing conditions, capital intensity, scale-up risk, regulation, and commercialization timing interacted. They do not establish one consensus causal ranking. (GFI Cultivated and Fermentation reports, advocacy, Apr. 2026, pp. 8-15; Meatable, company statement, 2025; Institute of Food Technologists, professional press, 23 Mar. 2026)

What is contested

Cultivated-meat cost curves remain unusually assumption-sensitive. CE Delft’s 2021 techno-economic analysis was commissioned by GFI and drew on confidential industry data; it modeled a path to competitive costs under ambitious process assumptions. David Humbird’s independent 2021 engineering analysis modeled stricter mass-transfer, media, and facility constraints and produced much less favorable industrial economics. They are not cleanly comparable because their process configurations, product specifications, cost boundaries, and assumed technological progress differ. (CE Delft, GFI-commissioned report, Mar. 2021; Humbird, engineering preprint, 2021)

Media-cost headlines are a recurrent provenance problem. Company announcements may quote dollars per litre, but product concentration, growth-factor composition, media consumption per kilogram, recycling, and downstream losses determine meat cost. The June 2026 Rethink Priorities review found no verified evidence that AI had already caused major media-cost reductions. A low per-litre quote is not an independently validated cost of meat. (Rethink Priorities, original analysis, 9 Jun. 2026; inference)

The market story differs by definition. Broad plant-based categories grew 3 percent in nominal global value in 2025, while United States plant-based meat and seafood sales continued to contract. A claim that “plant-based is growing” can be true for global dairy-adjacent categories and false for a specific meat category, geography, unit measure, or retailer panel. (GFI Plant-Based report, advocacy using Euromonitor and SPINS, Apr. 2026, p. 17; GFI/SPINS US retail analysis, advocacy/market data, 2026)

What is missing or unclear

There is no comparable, audited global revenue series for cultivated meat, precision-fermented food ingredients, and biomass-fermented products. Consultant market-size estimates typically mix addressable markets, forecast revenue, and proprietary definitions. They should not be put beside observed plant-based retail sales as if they measure the same thing. (GFI reports, advocacy, Apr. 2026; inference from disclosed methods)

Firm-level scale claims are rarely reported with the variables needed to test industrial economics: sustained batch yield, contamination rate, run length, vessel utilization, downstream recovery, energy use, food-grade depreciation, contracted offtake, and realized selling price. Announced facility capacity is usually nameplate or aspirational capacity, not verified annual output. (Rethink Priorities, original analysis, 9 Jun. 2026; GFI reports, advocacy, Apr. 2026; inference)

The evidence does not establish a single explanation for shutdowns. A defensible causal analysis would require company-level financing histories, burn, approval timing, facility commitments, unit economics, customer retention, and counterfactual access to capital. Bankruptcy filings and asset-sale documents are more informative than founders’ retrospective summaries, but no comprehensive coded dataset was found. (Green Queen, trade tracker, 16 Jun. 2026; LifeMate, independent industry analysis, Aug. 2026; inference)

CLAIM AUDIT Three commonly repeated statements require correction. “Below $1bn in 2026” refers to $881m invested during 2025. “70+ shut down” combines acquisitions, mergers, insolvencies, and closures since September 2024 and is not a GFI statistic. “Europe up 56%” refers to private investment in H1 2026, concentrated in fermentation, while public R&I funding fell in 2025.

Private finance is now thinner and more concentrated. In 2025, plant-based firms raised $450 million including public-company financing; cultivated firms raised $73.9 million, down from $144 million in 2024; fermentation firms raised $357 million, down from $651 million. Large transactions included EVERY’s $55 million round, Beyond Meat’s $100 million debt financing, and Aleph Farms’ $29 million round. Debt and public-company instruments are not equivalent to venture equity, so totals should not be interpreted as a uniform signal of investor risk appetite. (GFI Plant-Based, Cultivated, and Fermentation reports, advocacy using Net Zero Insights, Apr. 2026, pp. 30-33, 15, and 14)

Recurring specialist private funders include Agronomics, Big Idea Ventures, SOSV/IndieBio, Blue Horizon, CPT Capital, and S2G Ventures; sovereign and strategic investors such as Temasek, ADM, Cargill, JBS, and Tyson have also participated at different points. Participation is not durable commitment: several strategic incumbents have written down, restructured, or narrowed exposures. Current holdings and follow-on behavior therefore matter more than historical logos on cap tables. By the end of 2025, GFI identified at least $2.5 billion in cumulative public commitments across 33 countries, up from 16 countries in 2021. This is a nominal, cumulative, minimum figure. It mixes years and instruments and excludes some undisclosed or hard-to-attribute spending, so it cannot establish that public funding is replacing private capital dollar for dollar. (GFI State of Global Policy, advocacy, Apr. 2026, p. 3)

European public research and innovation funding was €236 million in 2025 and about €1.3 billion cumulatively from 2020 through 2025, according to GFI Europe’s grant tracker. The annual series fell in 2025. This is distinct from the €236 million of private investment reported for the first half of 2026; the matching number is coincidental. (GFI Europe research ecosystem tracker, advocacy, 2026; GFI Europe private-investment update, advocacy, 27 Aug. 2026)

Coefficient Giving, formerly Open Philanthropy, funds farmed-animal welfare and precompetitive alternatives-to-animal-products research. Its 2026 request for proposals anticipates up to $30 million across two rounds for off-flavour reduction, fats, egg replacement, and fish flavour, with roughly $100,000-$1 million awards in the first round and $1-$5 million in the second. The public programme materials do not provide a comprehensive “will not fund” list; absence from the call is not evidence of an explicit refusal. (Coefficient Giving, philanthropic programme and RFP, 2026)

The Bezos Earth Fund announced a $100 million sustainable-protein initiative in March 2024, including an initial $60 million for research centres. It later announced a $30 million centre at the National University of Singapore, alongside centres at Imperial College London and North Carolina State University. The stated remit is research and commercialization aimed at taste, cost, nutrition, and scale. The fund’s public pages do not publish a granular exclusion policy for protein grants. (Bezos Earth Fund, philanthropic announcements, 12 Mar. and 5 Sep. 2024)

Other philanthropic actors include New Harvest, which funds open cultivated-meat research; GFI’s research-grant programme; the Animal Welfare Fund; and Animal Charity Evaluators’ movement grants. Their intervention logics differ: scientific field building, nonprofit capacity, advocacy, or animal-welfare movement support. Aggregating them as “alt-protein philanthropy” obscures who receives funds and which outcomes are being purchased. (Funder programme pages, philanthropic/advocacy, current 2026; inference)

What is contested

One interpretation is that public capital is counter-cyclical and is filling gaps left by venture retreat, particularly in shared research, pilots, and regulatory capacity. The opposing interpretation is that cumulative commitments and announced programmes exaggerate substitution because budgets are multi-year, loans are repayable, broader bioeconomy facilities are only partly relevant, and private funding has fallen faster than public programmes can deploy. Current data establish diversification of funders, not replacement equivalence. (GFI State of Global Policy, advocacy, Apr. 2026; GFI Europe R&I tracker, advocacy, 2026; inference)

A second dispute concerns whether the capital shortfall is cyclical or reflects failed sector economics. Lower interest in high-burn food tech is a macro financing story; shutdowns after approval, facility delays, and weak repeat purchase point to technology- and category-specific problems. Both mechanisms are visible. The public record does not identify their relative contribution across firms. (GFI reports, advocacy, Apr. 2026; Institute of Food Technologists, professional press, 23 Mar. 2026; company statements, 2025-2026)

What is missing or unclear

No consolidated dataset reconciles commitment, award, disbursement, loan drawdown, and spend by technology track and policy function. Cross-country rankings based on announced commitments are therefore unstable. The missing unit of analysis is the funded project-year with instrument, recipient, purpose, disbursement, and co-financing fields. (GFI methodology caveats, advocacy, Apr. 2026; inference)

Private databases capture disclosed deals better than internal corporate R&D, strategic joint ventures, project finance, and incumbent plant conversions. Philanthropic databases describe grants better than declined proposals or opportunity cost. Claims about the “marginal funder” cannot be resolved from totals alone. (GFI investment methodology, advocacy, 2026; funder programme pages, 2026; inference)

3. Talent, labour, and computational R&D

FINDING Public evidence supports a narrower claim than “AI moves the bottleneck to computational talent.” Computational tools are already used in parts of protein R&D, but adoption is uneven and validation is sparse. Data access, bioprocess infrastructure, food science, regulation, manufacturing, and commercial execution remain co-binding constraints.

What is established

AltProtein.Jobs, operated by Tälist, is a specialist vacancy and candidate platform. Its 2025 career and hiring report says it analyzes thousands of job listings and applications from January through December 2025 across technology, product type, function, geography, seniority, and work arrangement. Public summaries mention demand in cell biology, automation, software, legal, manufacturing, sales, and marketing. The underlying row-level dataset and full codebook were not publicly accessible during this review, so exact distributions are platform claims rather than independently reproducible findings. (AltProtein.Jobs/Tälist, company/platform report, 2026)

If access opens, the data could directly describe posting volume, occupation, seniority, geography, employer, technology track, employment type, remote or hybrid status, application volume, and posting duration. It would show time-to-fill only if Tälist records a verified hire or closure event and distinguishes filled roles from expired, withdrawn, or duplicated postings. Vacancy text alone cannot establish hiring difficulty. (AltProtein.Jobs report description, company/platform, 2026; inference)

The best cross-track skills evidence remains GFI’s 2022 survey of 130 startups, 48 percent of them very early stage. Respondents emphasized food and meat science, process development and scale-up, manufacturing, product development, and food safety. Cultivated firms highlighted bioreactors, scaffolding, media, cell lines, and tissue engineering; fermentation firms highlighted strain development and downstream processing. Because the respondent frame is advocacy-network startups and self-reported unmet need, it measures perceived demand rather than verified vacancies or wages. (GFI startup survey, advocacy, 2022)

GFI India’s skills-mapping work similarly reports gaps in hands-on bioprocessing, cell culture, extrusion, analytical methods, and novel-food regulation despite a large graduate pool. The project was commissioned by GFI India and conducted with Idobro; its jurisdictional and sponsor limits matter. The consistent cross-source signal is a shortage of people who can translate laboratory science into food-grade processes, not a generic shortage of STEM graduates. (GFI India/Idobro, commissioned advocacy research, 2025-2026; inference)

Competition with adjacent sectors is role-specific. Cultivated meat and precision fermentation draw from biopharma and industrial biotechnology for cell culture, fermentation, quality assurance, validation, and regulatory affairs; plant-based firms compete with conventional food manufacturers for sensory science, extrusion, formulation, operations, and retail commercialization. Pharma can usually offer clearer career ladders and higher-margin economics, while food manufacturing offers directly relevant scale and safety experience. Public data do not quantify the resulting wage premium or attrition rate. (GFI survey, advocacy, 2022; AltProtein.Jobs, company/platform, 2026; inference)

What is contested

The strongest pro-AI position is that biological design spaces are large, experiments are expensive, and active learning can reduce wet-lab iterations in media, cell lines, strain design, and ingredient discovery. The limiting case is that models need standardized, sufficiently large, shareable datasets tied to comparable outcomes. In a young sector, those data are scarce, proprietary, and generated on different platforms. The model can optimize the wrong objective faster if the assay does not predict industrial performance. (Frontiers in Artificial Intelligence, peer-reviewed review, 2024; Rethink Priorities, original analysis, 9 Jun. 2026)

The “bottleneck shift” hypothesis is further undercut by sequence. If unit economics improve, the Rethink Priorities study expects infrastructure and scale-up to become more binding, not less. Computation cannot by itself supply vessels, validate aseptic food production, finance facilities, obtain approvals, or generate repeat consumer demand. Conversely, better experimental design could lower R&D cost at the margin without becoming the single sectoral bottleneck. (Rethink Priorities, original analysis, 9 Jun. 2026; inference)

AI advantage is also not exclusive to alternatives. Conventional animal agriculture, feed, breeding, and processing have larger operating datasets, established sensor infrastructure, and incumbent balance sheets. A general improvement in biological modeling could strengthen incumbents as readily as entrants. Transition effects depend on relative adoption, complementary assets, and regulation, not raw model capability alone. (Rethink Priorities, original analysis, 9 Jun. 2026; inference)

What is missing or unclear

No public labour-market series combines vacancies, applicants, hires, wages, vacancy duration, firm stage, technology track, and closure outcomes. LinkedIn, Tälist, and startup surveys each observe a selected slice. Without denominator data on firms and employment, “fastest growing function” cannot be stated reliably for the sector as a whole. (AltProtein.Jobs, company/platform, 2026; GFI, advocacy survey, 2022; inference)

No public benchmark measures model-to-lab-to-pilot gains across firms. The useful outcome variables would include experiments avoided, time to validated candidate, improvement under scale-up, cost per successful run, and failure rate compared with a non-AI baseline. Company case studies usually report candidate discovery or model accuracy, not these counterfactual outcomes. (Rethink Priorities, original analysis, 9 Jun. 2026; peer-reviewed reviews, 2024-2026; inference)

What is contested

Proponents of state bans frame them as consumer protection, agricultural defense, or precaution; opponents frame them as protectionist restrictions that conflict with federal safety oversight and interstate commerce. The legal question is not exhausted by food safety. It includes preemption, dormant Commerce Clause doctrine, compelled or restricted commercial speech, and the relation between a state sales ban and federally inspected products. (state statutes and litigation, official, 2024-2026; Duke World Food Policy Center report, peer-reviewed/policy research, 2026)

European review-time claims are often misleading. The formal EFSA assessment clock excludes periods when the applicant must provide more information, and authorization adds Commission and member-state stages. Advocates emphasize that clock-stops make elapsed time longer and less predictable; regulators emphasize that applicant completeness and product novelty drive requests. Public case data are still too sparse to estimate a stable median time for cultivated products. (EFSA novel-food guidance, official, current 2026; UK FSA sandbox report, official, Jul. 2026; inference)

What is missing or unclear

No official cross-jurisdiction database uses common fields for application date, validation, clock-stops, safety opinion, authorization, facility inspection, label approval, launch, and withdrawal. Sector trackers often collapse these stages. A clean comparison requires product-level event histories. (regulator inventories and sector trackers, 2024-2026; inference)

China’s path remains particularly opaque in English-language public materials. The unresolved questions are competent authority, dossier standard, whether genetically modified inputs trigger additional review, and how national planning translates into product authorization. These should be checked against Chinese-language National Health Commission and State Administration for Market Regulation materials before making a stronger claim. (GFI State of Global Policy, advocacy, Apr. 2026; inference)

5. Foresight and scenario practice

Food-systems foresight is a mature family of practices with weak standardization. The dominant qualitative tradition is participatory systems change; the dominant quantitative tradition is global land-use, commodity, and emissions modeling. Neither automatically represents firm strategy, political opposition, consumer identity, regulatory discontinuities, or the industrial learning curves central to protein transitions. (Foresight4Food/UN Food Systems Coordination Hub guide, practitioner, Jul. 2025; Pedde et al., peer-reviewed review, 2025; inference)

What is established

The document often called “FAO’s foresight guide” is more precisely Using Foresight for Food Systems Transformation, Version 1.0, produced by Foresight4Food and the UN Food Systems Coordination Hub and hosted by FAO. It was published in July 2025 with programme support from the Netherlands through IFAD and from a Germany-funded youth programme. That institutional origin matters: it is a practitioner guide for inclusive transformation processes, not an FAO forecasting standard. (Foresight4Food and UN Food Systems Coordination Hub, practitioner guide, Jul. 2025, front matter)

The guide uses four iterative phases. “Scope” establishes purpose, users, process, governance, resources, and participation. “Map” describes system actors, outcomes, power, drivers, feedbacks, and evidence. “Explore” combines trends, drivers, critical uncertainties, scenarios, implications, and often backcasting. “Mobilise” connects a preferred future to pathways, alliances, experiments, and adaptive management. Cross-cutting requirements are participation, evidence, learning, and institutional embedding. (Foresight4Food/UN guide, practitioner, Jul. 2025, pp. 10-29)

Its toolset includes stakeholder mapping, rich pictures, causal-loop work, political-economy and power analysis, STEEPLE scanning, impact-uncertainty matrices, horizon scanning, causal layered analysis, futures wheels, role play, Three Horizons, simulations, visioning, theory of change, and backcasting. The guide explicitly rejects a single standardized sequence. It also warns against nominal participation, expert presentations replacing dialogue, stakeholder domination, one-off consultant exercises, and failure to embed foresight in decision processes. (Foresight4Food/UN guide, practitioner, Jul. 2025, pp. 26-32)

Foresight4Food is a network and practice platform linked to Oxford and Wageningen researchers and partners. Its Foresight for Food Systems Transformation initiative worked in Bangladesh, Jordan, Kenya, and Uganda from 2022 to 2025 with support from the Netherlands and IFAD. A 2026 quality guide sets nine criteria around purpose, systems framing, evidence, participation, futures diversity, pathways, learning, ownership, and influence. The network’s known limitations are partly self-diagnosed: uneven ownership, weak policy uptake, technical framings that evade values and power, and scenario exercises that remain incremental. (Foresight4Food programme pages, practitioner network, 2022-2026; Criteria for High-Quality Foresight, practitioner guide, Feb. 2026)

The examples in the table show that none of the named methods would be wholly novel in food or agriculture. What is rarer is a traceable application to the AI-protein intersection with explicit falsifiers and monitoring rules. The direct Shell lineage is especially thin: four-quadrant food scenarios are common, but citations often point to generic scenario-planning practice rather than Shell’s institutional method. (peer-reviewed and programme examples, 2005-2026; inference)

The 2025 Agricultural Systems review identified 36 quantitative food-system scenario studies and classified four common designs: a single normative scenario testing feasibility; multiple normative scenarios testing alternative conditions; a single exploratory variable; and exploratory scenarios combining multiple variables. The review concludes that scenario type and model architecture shape what questions can be answered. Its sample was assembled through a structured search and snowballing, not a census of all modeling practice. (Pedde et al., peer-reviewed Agricultural Systems review, 2025)

GLOBIOM is a partial-equilibrium model covering agriculture, forestry, bioenergy, trade, land use, production systems, and emissions with spatial detail. MAgPIE is a global land-use optimization model coupled to biophysical crop and vegetation outputs. Both are strong for internally consistent commodity, land, production, trade, and emissions responses to exogenous assumptions. They represent market actors through optimization and equilibrium abstractions. Power, corporate strategy, regulatory sequence, heterogeneous food identity, and discontinuous firm entry are weak unless introduced externally. (GLOBIOM official documentation, current 2026; MAgPIE official repository/documentation, current 2026; Pedde et al., peer-reviewed, 2025; inference)

Early-warning mechanics across adjacent fields

Nonproliferation monitoring begins with an acquisition path: the sequence of capabilities, materials, equipment, facilities, knowledge, and activities required to reach a prohibited state. Indicators are observable traces tied to nodes on that path. IAEA guidance groups them across equipment, nuclear and non-nuclear materials, technology, training, research, effluent, and safety measures, then combines information to test consistency with declared activity. (IAEA Nuclear Verification Series, official guidance, 2010s edition current online in 2026)

Biosecurity systems use event definitions, baseline incidence, sentinel data streams, trigger thresholds, validation, escalation ownership, and periodic recalibration. A 2026 WHO-PREZODE indicator project combined expert selection with empirical testing against avian-influenza and MERS events. The important transfer is the chain from causal risk factor to observable signal to defined response, not the use of a dashboard. (WHO/PREZODE, official technical product, 2026)

AI compute-monitoring proposals focus on observables that are necessary, detectable, hard to substitute, and quantifiable: advanced chips, power and data-centre infrastructure, cloud records, training-run attestations, and site inspection. A June 2025 UN Scientific Advisory Board paper frames verification as combining self-report, hardware and location information, data and safety protocols, and independent checks. The architecture works best when a concentrated physical input creates a choke point. (UN Scientific Advisory Board, official expert paper, Jun. 2025)

Adapting the mechanics to protein would require a decision question, separate acquisition paths by technology, variables with stable definitions, data owners, collection cadence, baselines, thresholds, false-positive and false-negative analysis, a review trigger, and periodic recalibration. Candidate observables include regulatory filings, financial close on facilities, installed versus utilized capacity, sustained batch performance, contracted offtake, realized price gaps, repeat purchase, company exits, state bans, and role-specific vacancies. These are conditions for adaptation, not a recommendation to build an indicator system. (IAEA, WHO/PREZODE, UN AI-verification sources, 2025-2026; inference)

The transfer limit is structural. Protein transitions lack a single concentrated input comparable to fissile material or frontier chips. Production routes substitute for one another, firms keep process data private, consumer demand can reverse, and regulation is fragmented. Indicators therefore need track-specific causal logic and corroboration across independent data streams. A generic “investment/jobs/patents” index would be easy to update and hard to interpret. (adjacent-field sources, 2025-2026; inference)

What is contested

Participatory foresight advocates treat legitimacy, inclusion, and shared learning as substantive outputs. Critics point out that participation can launder a preselected transformation agenda or give organized stakeholders veto power over uncomfortable scenarios. The Foresight4Food guide acknowledges power and ownership problems but offers no external validation standard for whether a scenario set predicts, discriminates, or changes decisions. (Foresight4Food/UN guide, practitioner, Jul. 2025; Foresight4Food quality guide, practitioner, Feb. 2026; inference)

Quantitative models provide discipline and reproducibility, but they can turn political choices into exogenous parameters and optimize within an incumbent ontology. Qualitative scenarios represent institutional discontinuity and meaning better, but often lack calibrated magnitudes. Hybrid modeling does not eliminate the tradeoff; it makes the translation assumptions more visible if documented. (Pedde et al., peer-reviewed, 2025; GLOBIOM and MAgPIE documentation, 2026; inference)

What is missing or unclear

The protein evidence base lacks open time series for the technical variables that would discriminate among scale-up scenarios. Regulatory events and funding rounds are visible; realized production, yields, unit economics, repeat purchase, and facility utilization are not. This supports qualitative branching and structured elicitation more strongly than calibrated forecasting. (Sections 1-4 evidence; inference)

Published food-systems applications rarely report whether an indicator triggered a decision, whether the decision improved outcomes, or how false alarms were handled. Early-warning evaluation is therefore a missing empirical layer, not merely a design detail. (Foresight4Food case materials, 2022-2026; Pedde et al., 2025; inference)

What is contested

The absence claim is method-dependent. Working terminology

Alternative protein. An umbrella for products intended to replace or displace conventional animal proteins; category boundaries vary by source. (GFI State of Industry reports, advocacy, Apr. 2026)

Plant-based. Foods formulated from plant-derived proteins, fats, carbohydrates, and flavours, often structured to reproduce animal-product functions. (GFI Plant-Based report, advocacy, Apr. 2026)

Biomass fermentation. Production in which the microbial cells or mycelium are the main food biomass. (GFI Fermentation report, advocacy, Apr. 2026)

Precision fermentation. Use of a microorganism, often engineered, to produce a specific target molecule such as a protein or fat. (GFI Fermentation report, advocacy, Apr. 2026)

Cultivated meat. Food produced from animal cells grown outside an animal; “cell-cultivated,” “cultured,” and “cell-based” are regulatory or political variants. (FDA and USDA-FSIS, official, current 2026; GFI Cultivated report, advocacy, Apr. 2026)

Novel food. A legal category for foods without the required history of consumption or with specified novel production features; it is not a synonym for cultivated meat. (EFSA and Singapore SFA, official guidance, current 2026)

Techno-economic analysis. An engineering and cost model linking process assumptions to capital and operating costs. Results are scenarios, not audited future prices. (CE Delft, GFI-commissioned analysis, 2021; Humbird, engineering preprint, 2021)

Nameplate capacity. Maximum designed output under stated assumptions. It is not actual production, utilization, or sold volume. (engineering usage; applied here as an inference, 2026)

Precision / uncertainty. Risk permits probability distributions that can be estimated; Knightian uncertainty concerns materially unknown states or probabilities. Protein-transition data contain both. (conceptual distinction; application to evidence in Sections 1-5 is this briefing’s inference, 2026)

Evidence limits

This briefing is current to 5 September 2026. Regulatory dockets, company status, funding rounds, and programme budgets can change quickly. The strongest evidence gaps are firm-level production economics, comparable labour-market outcomes, project-level public disbursement, and outcome evaluation for foresight exercises. Those gaps are reported rather than filled with consultant market forecasts or unverified company claims. (evidence synthesis from Sections 1-6; inference, 5 Sep. 2026)
