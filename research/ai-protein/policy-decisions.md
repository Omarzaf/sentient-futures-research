# Policy Evidence-to-Decision Table

> Earlier background research. AI-assisted draft; human verification pending. This is not the current India–Pakistan study.

**Proposed policy discussion draft — not validated findings.** This converts the reviewed public-source register and literature review into bounded policy choices. It does not rank spending, set thresholds, or claim that any forecast panel has been run. It extends the policy inventory in the [FEASTS German National Action Plan](https://www.cell-ag.de/_files/ugd/cd7689_34c289309fbd4cdbae0a16acbbb40967.pdf), coauthored by Liliia Lisina and Pia Voltz, by specifying evidence that would favor or alter each choice. The plan is a stakeholder research contribution, not an adopted government policy.

| Decision area | Policy choice for discussion | Evidence already available | Missing discriminator / indicator | Conditions favoring support | Reason to change course | Animal / regional outcome and team interface |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Public meals and institutional procurement | Fund real-world public-institution trials of defaults, menu design, pricing, and procurement contracts for plant-based or fermentation-based meals. | Garnett et al. found a cafeteria price change increased vegetarian sales but did not clearly reduce overall meat sales ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvp.2021.101589)). Ginn and Sparkman found vegan defaults shifted campus dining choices ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvp.2023.102226)). | Actual replacement rather than trial uptake: animal-product servings, waste, nutrition, satisfaction, repeat choice, and whether diners substitute elsewhere. | Support when the intervention measures purchases or servings at the point of choice, protects meal quality and nutrition, and reports animal-product sales or servings, not only alternative-protein uptake. | Change course if uptake is novelty-driven, waste rises, nutrition or satisfaction deteriorates, or meat, fish, egg, or dairy servings simply move to another station or meal. | The measured near-term outcome is category-specific servings replaced; animal effects additionally require evidence about production response. Interface: adoption evaluation, procurement, nutrition, and animal-impact accounting. |
| 2. Retail substitution and price policy | Treat price parity, product improvement, tax incentives, or retail support as conditional on measured displacement by product category. | Neuhofer and Lusk show most plant-based-meat buyers also buy ground meat ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-16996-5)). Lusk et al. model small cattle-production effects from plant-based-meat price or demand shifts ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ac4fda)). Lee and Sumner estimate a plant-based-milk replacement relationship in a static U.S. fluid-milk demand model ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306919226000862)). | Scanner-based substitution by category, repeat buying, total protein volume, import/export leakage, and animal-production response. | Support where evidence shows the alternative is replacing the target animal product, with a stated counterfactual and product boundary. Milk and meat studies do not establish comparable universal coefficients. | Change course if sales are additive, if consumers switch from beef to chicken or fish, or if farm-level production does not respond. | Outcome is fewer animals only after retail displacement reaches upstream production. Interface: demand modeling, retail data, species-specific animal accounting, and regional market analysis. |
| 3. AI-assisted cultivated-meat and fermentation R&D | Support open, milestone-based R&D for media, cell lines, bioprocess control, and fermentation where AI accelerates wet-lab learning. | Humbird identifies scale-up constraints in cultured meat ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.27848)). Risner et al. warn that refined media can make modeled cultured meat environmentally costly ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsfoodscitech.4c00281)). Sinke et al. show climate advantages depend on renewable energy and medium assumptions ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11367-022-02128-8)). Cosenza et al. demonstrate Bayesian serum-free media search, with caveats ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/elsc.202300005)). | Public indicators for food-grade animal-component-free media, cell density, doubling time, contamination control, energy source, finished-product functionality, and transparent TEA/LCA assumptions. | Support when AI is tied to wet-lab validation, open datasets, multi-objective optimization including cost and environmental impact, and clear species/cell-type endpoints. | Revise the funding stage or conditions if promised validation fails, relevant cost or environmental tradeoffs are excluded, or refinement assumptions prove infeasible. Serum-free work can still be a legitimate intermediate milestone. | Animal effects remain conditional on affordable production, adoption and substitution. Assess local energy, manufacturing capacity and who can access the technology. Interface: technical R&D, LCA/TEA, forecasting, and public-good data. |
| 4. Regulatory and safety capacity | Invest in transparent safety review capacity, dossier guidance, and jurisdictional learning before assuming rapid approvals or global rollout. | FDA completed a U.S. pre-market consultation for UPSIDE Foods ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/food/hfp-constituent-updates/fda-completes-first-pre-market-consultation-human-food-made-using-animal-cell-culture-technology)); Singapore approved cultured chicken through its novel-food pathway ([MSE Singapore](https://www.mse.gov.sg/latest-news/newsletter-climate-action-in-sg-dec/)); the EU uses Novel Food Regulation 2015/2283 ([EC](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/novel-food/legislation_en)); FAO/WHO summarize cell-based-food safety issues ([WHO/FAO](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240070943)). | Review timelines, safety data norms, labeling rules, monitoring, and whether small firms or Global South producers can comply. | Support when public agencies publish clear requirements, recognize uncertainty, and build interoperable safety evidence without lowering standards. | Change course if approval becomes a bottleneck only large incumbents can navigate, or if safety, labeling, or trust failures dominate adoption. | Regional outcome is uneven market access and regulatory capacity. Interface: legal/regulatory review, safety science, consumer trust, and international comparison. |
| 5. Fair transition and animal-impact accounting | Pair alternative-protein policy with livelihood assessment and species-specific animal accounting rather than treating tons of meat as the only outcome. | FAO emphasizes livestock’s roles in poverty reduction and livelihoods ([FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/i2744e/i2744e00.htm)). Mood et al. estimate very large numbers of farmed fishes killed for food ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/awf.2023.4)). OECD-FAO meat outlooks show continued regional meat-market dynamics ([OECD-FAO](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-fao-agricultural-outlook-2026-2035_47874669-en/full-report/meat_149b4ca3.html)). | Who gains or loses income, which animal species are affected, whether substitution increases small-bodied-animal consumption, and whether regional food security worsens. | Support where policy includes farmer diversification, workforce skills, local crop or fermentation inputs, and animal counts by species alongside climate and nutrition metrics. | Change course if transition policy harms vulnerable producers, worsens protein access, or reduces beef while increasing chicken, fish, or shrimp deaths. | Outcome combines livelihoods and sentient-animal counts, not just kilograms. Interface: regional equity, animal welfare, political economy, and measurement design. |
| 6. Forecasting and adaptive governance | Use AI-assisted forecasting as a decision-support protocol, not as a proxy for validated human superforecasting. | Halawi et al. approach human crowd forecasting with LLM systems ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563)); Schoenegger et al. report strong LLM-ensemble accuracy but calibration concerns ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adp1528)); Park et al. warn about diminished diversity of thought ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-023-02307-x)); Mellers et al. document cultivated human superforecasters ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615577794)); IPCC distinguishes scenarios from predictions ([IPCC](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/annex-ii/)). | Calibration, Brier scores, leakage control, dated retrieval, pairwise error correlation across models, independent pre-deliberation forecasts, and human baseline comparisons. | Support when the team records assumptions, preserves pre-deliberation rounds, separates scenarios from forecasts, and uses LLMs to surface evidence and arguments before scored human judgment. | Change course if model errors are correlated, source weighting is mistaken for forecast skill, or quantile spreads are reported as calibrated prediction intervals. | The intended benefit is better timing and explicit review triggers; this remains an evaluation question, not a demonstrated animal outcome. Interface: forecasting protocol, methods review, evidence synthesis, and adaptive-policy triggers. |

**AI-use disclosure:** this table was drafted with AI assistance from reviewed public registers and literature-review notes. Human verification of citations and interpretations is still pending before it should be treated as validated project material.
