# Household meals and protein substitution

Research agenda only. No test below has been estimated. Human verification pending.

## Hypothesis

Alternative products may gain repeated use when they fit an affordable shared household meal, rather than when an individual merely expresses willingness to try them. Preparation, household size, meal sharing and differences in dietary practice could affect that fit. This is a hypothesis, not an observed result for Pakistan or India.

The competing explanation is that price, income, availability and taste account for the pattern without a separate household-meal mechanism. National protein supply, stated acceptance and a product launch cannot distinguish these explanations.

## Test one Household meal structure and conventional protein purchases

Use HCES household food quantities, expenditure, household size and the roster's meals-at-home fields only after the official reproduction gate passes. Define the meal-sharing exposure and a matched animal-protein budget-share outcome before fitting. Adjust for expenditure, residence, geography, household composition and defensible local price measures. Test whether the association changes across meal-sharing and household-size groups.

A disappearance of the association after those controls weakens the proposed meal mechanism. This analysis concerns conventional food purchases. HCES item categories do not automatically identify branded alternative products, so it cannot establish alternative-protein adoption. Keep household and individual measurement levels explicit.

## Test two Cost of a complete comparable meal

Construct locally relevant recipe baskets from documented ingredient quantities and dated retail prices. Match portions, protein and meal function; retain energy and other nutritional differences rather than describing protein equivalence as overall nutritional equivalence. Compare a conventional meal, a familiar pulse-based meal and an alternative-product meal where the product is actually listed.

Use ingredient prices and quantities with the same raw or cooked basis. Cooking yields, fuel and preparation time are additional inputs; missing inputs remain unknown. Price per gram of packaged protein alone does not measure the cost of a cooked household meal. Evidence of a premium that disappears in the complete basket would weaken the simple packaged-product affordability explanation. A robust full-meal premium would support further affordability research, not prove a causal adoption effect.

## Test three Dietary practice after economic and geographic adjustment

Use the specified NFHS-5 respondent universe to model reported weekly animal-food consumption with religion, caste, wealth, state and residence. Report design-based confidence intervals and a prespecified contribution-decomposition method. Add available demographic controls and show how results change with their inclusion.

Differences that disappear after wealth, geography and residence adjustment weaken an interpretation based solely on religious or caste identity. Surviving associations do not by themselves identify a shared-meal mechanism, individual nutrient intake, purchase behaviour or causal effects. Do not link NFHS and HCES respondents by geographic similarity or treat them as one panel.

## Test four Change within a matched category over time

If existing household purchase panels or comparable repeated surveys become lawfully accessible, examine category entry, repeat purchases and changes in matched animal-product purchases. Specify product classification, timing, prices and an untreated comparison before inspecting effects. Test pretrends and sensitivity to household composition and availability.

The null or falsifier is that alternative-product entry adds spending without a corresponding change in matched animal-product purchases, or that an apparent substitution pattern disappears after price and availability adjustment. Retail listings alone cannot measure household repeat purchase or displacement. No new human respondent survey is authorized by this proposal.

## Test five Institutions and the meal format

Where public archival menus, procurement quantities and expenditure records exist, compare whether new products enter shared dishes, stand-alone portions or snacks. Record the institution, dates, quantities, price basis and the population actually served. Establish whether a documented procurement change represents repeated use or a one-off demonstration.

An institutional menu is evidence about that institution, not a national household adoption rate. Certification or a procurement announcement alone is not evidence of consumption. This test remains unavailable until suitable records and a defensible comparison exist.

## Acceptance and reporting

Preregister each executable test's outcome, unit, exclusions, control set and falsifier before estimation. Do not search for a model that makes the household-meal hypothesis appear true. Report null and conflicting findings, measurement gaps and alternative explanations. Keep all five proposals separate from the current observed results and forecasts.
