# Halal decisions require named authorities and traceable processes

AI-assisted research draft, 15 October 2026. The author has checked and accepted the existing Urdu/Arabic translations and religious interpretations; [the review receipt](author-review.json) binds that acceptance to this exact edition. No independent specialist credential or certified translation is claimed. The claim and source ledgers preserve their original pre-review statuses; the receipt records the subsequent author review. These are attributed documentary findings, with original passages in [the claim ledger](claims.json) and provenance in [the source register](source-register.json). No country-wide religious consensus, product certification, legal access or market demand follows from these sources.

Pakistan's Banuri Town Darul Iftaa supplies two specific laboratory-meat answers. The 31 October 2018 answer makes permissibility conditional on ingredients, production conditions and health. The 17 September 2019 answer lists conditions and recommends avoidance until they are known. The latter question groups plant and animal routes, so its conditions must retain their route-specific scope. These answers establish attributed positions at one Karachi institution; their dates and differing formulations do not establish a national change of doctrine. [2018 answer](https://www.banuri.edu.pk/readquestion/144001200799/31-10-2018), [2019 answer](https://www.banuri.edu.pk/readquestion/144012201327/17-09-2019). Claims: `HALAL-PK-BANURI-2018`, `HALAL-PK-BANURI-2019`.

A second Pakistani source is Muhammad Muaz Ashraf's analysis hosted by Jamia Darul Uloom Karachi. Its Urdu and Arabic versions carry an introductory endorsement from Muhammad Taqi Usmani mentioning consultation. The concluding conditions distinguish cells from lawful slaughtered animals, an exception for parts that do not bear life, and prohibited admixtures. The discussion does not simply presume that processing establishes *istihala*. These are two language witnesses of one authored analysis, rather than two independent authorities. The landing post is dated 17 October 2023; the Urdu installments display November and December 2023 issue dates. No exact issuance day is assigned to the entire work. [Institutional post](https://darululoomkarachi.edu.pk/?p=30112), [Urdu original](https://darululoomkarachi.edu.pk/wp-content/uploads/lab-grown-meat-urdu.pdf), [Arabic original](https://darululoomkarachi.edu.pk/wp-content/uploads/lab-grown-meat-arabic.pdf). Claims: `HALAL-PK-DUK-ATTRIBUTION`, `HALAL-PK-DUK-PROCESS`.

The Arabic material does not substitute a Gulf council for a Pakistani institution. Karachi's Arabic article is locally authored and published. Banuri's Arabic passage is a classical quotation within its answer; the cited classical edition has not been independently collated. Neither source is a certificate for a named manufacturer's cells, media, scaffold or finished product.

India's Islamic Fiqh Academy provides an original Urdu certification resolution from its 1–3 March 2015 seminar in Ochira, Kerala, with an issuer English version. The resolution requires expertise, credible testing and continuing oversight. Its scope is general halal certification. It does not resolve the status of harvested animal-cell meat. The adopted text is on PDF page 19; the earlier questionnaire is a different documentary layer. [Urdu seminar original](https://www.ifa-india.org/ur/pdfs/pdf-20260729113303.pdf), [issuer English version](https://www.ifa-india.org/pdfs/pdf-20210410124823.pdf). Claim: `HALAL-IN-IFA-2015`.

The Academy's 9–11 November 2024 Bihar seminar also adopted general AI guidance. Its decision cautions about misleading generated information and fabricated references and requires further research. This supports a requirement for expert verification of AI-assisted religious research. It supplies no cultivated-food approval or authorization for automated halal certification. The AI questions on page 8 must not be substituted for the decisions on pages 14–15. [Urdu original](https://www.ifa-india.org/ur/pdfs/pdf-20260729113944.pdf). Claim: `HALAL-IN-IFA-AI-2024`.

Certification introduces another documentary layer. Jamiat Ulama-i-Hind Halal Trust's scheme, version 4 dated 31 October 2023, asks for composition, process and supplier evidence and describes a distinct review decision. Its broad food and biochemical categories do not demonstrate that it has certified a cultivated product. [Trust scheme](https://www.jamiathalaltrust.org/globals/SCH-2.pdf). In official registers, NABCB's PC038 entry concerns Halal India Pvt. Ltd. and slaughterhouse scope, while Pakistan Halal Authority lists registered certification bodies. Neither entry is a cultivated-product certificate. Dates, scope and the identity of the body therefore remain separate checks. [NABCB PC038](https://nabcb.qci.org.in/pc-038/), [PHA register](https://www.pakistanhalalauthority.gov.pk/rhcbs.aspx). Claims: `HALAL-IN-JUHHT-PROCESS`, `HALAL-REGISTERS-SCOPE`.

AI has a bounded technical role in the reviewed policy evidence: a historical DBT-BIRAC call identifies AI optimization of fermentation monitoring and predictive control as a research objective. A call establishes a proposed research direction; it does not establish an award, measured gain or halal compliance. [Original call, section 2.2.1](https://birac.nic.in/webcontent/1743051137_Approved_CFP_Biomanufacturing_Smart_Proteins.pdf). Claim: `HALAL-AI-DBT-OBJECTIVE`.

The proposed [signposts](signposts.json) consequently monitor identifiable certificates, explicit scope changes and documented AI project results. They supplement the existing regulatory questions; they do not measure sales or replace the survey's pillar/year cells. A halal certificate, a food-safety authorization and consumer adoption require separate evidence.

No Indian Arabic cultivated-specific original or named domestic cultivated-product certificate was established. These are corpus gaps, not claims that no ruling or certificate exists.
