## The comparison is informative but an adoption baseline is still missing

India and Pakistan differ in the food available nationally, the quantities recorded by household surveys and the institutions relevant to new food products. These differences identify questions for a comparative study. They do not establish which country will adopt alternative proteins faster, how many consumers will buy them, or whether purchases will displace animal foods.

The principal findings are:

- Pakistan's poultry availability was {{claim:fbs_2023_pakistan_poultry}} kg per person per year, compared with {{claim:fbs_2023_india_poultry}} in India, in {{year:2023}}. These are FAOSTAT food-balance quantities available for human consumption.
- India's pulse availability was {{claim:fbs_2023_india_pulses}} kg per person per year, compared with {{claim:fbs_2023_pakistan_pulses}} in Pakistan in the same year. Familiar plant foods therefore belong in a study of competing meals; pulse supply does not measure demand for a manufactured meat analogue.
- The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World (SOFI) estimates that {{claim:nutrition_2024_india_unaffordable}} percent of India's population and {{claim:nutrition_2024_pakistan_unaffordable}} percent of Pakistan's could not afford a healthy diet in {{year:2024}}. Economic access is a separate outcome from measured dietary intake.
- The selected Joint Child Malnutrition Estimates (JME) country model estimates under-five stunting at {{claim:nutrition_2024_india_stunting}} percent in India and {{claim:nutrition_2024_pakistan_stunting}} percent in Pakistan for {{year:2024}}. These modelled estimates cannot be added to unaffordability or undernourishment counts.
- India's official household-based protein estimates can be compared with adult requirement scenarios only after retaining the published consumer-unit denominator. The consumer unit is based on energy requirements, so this comparison cannot establish protein adequacy or population deficiency.
- Pakistan's previously displayed complete protein total is withdrawn. Named physical conversions now describe a partial food basket, with unresolved foods and denominator reconciliation left visible. No national adequacy estimate or rural and urban model ranking survives that repair.
- The supply benchmark predicts national protein availability under exploratory models. Its held-out interval results differ sharply between the country series, and its forecast intervals do not supply probabilities of alternative-protein adoption.
- The consumer-study register contains {{claim:package_p07_study_records}} studies and {{claim:package_p07_finding_records}} extracted findings. These are heterogeneous published results, not respondent microdata or a bilateral acceptance estimate.
- India has documented research support and food-regulatory instruments; Pakistan has halal institutions and a Punjab instrument containing a general novel-food provision. Neither a programme nor a rule establishes approval or sales of a named cultivated product.
- The halal work identifies product, process and institutional conditions. A religious position, a halal certificate and food authorization must each be established for the case being assessed.

The main unresolved empirical requirement is a compatible observation of consumer behaviour for a specified product, place and period. The current record does not provide that baseline. The most useful next work is to obtain eligible respondent data, verify actual product availability and test questions about price, meal use and repeat purchase before making an adoption forecast.

## A common meal makes the comparative question testable

::: {data-package="P01"}

The India–Pakistan brief asks when a new protein product earns a recurring place in a household meal. It distinguishes willingness to try from purchase, habitual use and the food displaced. That distinction remains the report's organizing question. National food supply describes the context in which people choose; it does not observe their response to a new product. The brief is a secondary synthesis, with no project consumer experiment or interview evidence.

The primary proposed comparison is a plant-based chicken product, a conventional chicken option and a familiar pulse-based meal. The options need specified serving sizes, prices, preparation demands and product descriptions. A cultivated-meat vignette would be analysed separately because its animal origin and production process introduce different questions. Precision-fermented ingredients and fungal biomass also require their own descriptions. A response to one category cannot stand for acceptance of all alternative proteins.

The brief's argument has an economic rival: price, taste and availability may account for differences that appear cultural. Comparable purchase conditions might reveal that differences within a country exceed those between selected groups in the two countries. Religious affiliation, urban residence or an aggregate food quantity cannot settle that question. The {{cite:package_P01|India–Pakistan research brief}} proposes a comparison that can distinguish these rival explanations.

:::

::: {data-package="P02"}

The foundational plan established an India–Pakistan research lane alongside support for forecast-question design. Its early policy-attention hypothesis was a proposal to investigate, not an empirical result. The later halal work expands the documented scope without cancelling the original comparative question. This report connects the workstreams through product-specific evidence while retaining their different methods.

A forecast question needs a named product category, geography, outcome, denominator, deadline and resolution source. Retail availability, repeated purchasing and animal-food displacement are different events. Where no compatible baseline or resolution source exists, the question remains a monitoring or data-acquisition task. Repeated model answers do not supply the missing observation, and disagreement between language models is not a calibrated probability interval.

## The drivers are hypotheses about decisions

Affordability concerns what a household can buy relative to familiar meals. The relevant comparison is a delivered serving at an actual outlet, including preparation and access costs where measured. A laboratory cost estimate or premium retail offer cannot establish a typical household's choice. The household expenditure gradients in this report motivate price-sensitive questions without identifying an elasticity for alternative proteins.

Dietary practice concerns ingredients, meal roles, preparation and the people who buy and cook. A product resembling meat may appeal to someone seeking a substitute and repel someone avoiding meat-like foods. Both possibilities fit the present evidence. A questionnaire should therefore ask what the proposed product would replace and whether it fits an ordinary meal, rather than treating resemblance as a universal advantage.

Social legitimacy concerns whose approval matters and how a choice is understood in the household or community. The report's culture matrix contains descriptive context, not a causal ranking of religions or nationalities. Region, income, caste, gender, access and dietary practice can overlap. Their independent contributions require a design that measures those relationships instead of assigning them from a national label.

Certification concerns the credibility and scope of an assurance for a named product. A consumer's belief, a religious authority's conditions and a certifier's decision are separate observations. For cultivated meat, donor history and culture inputs may matter as much as the final label. A generic assertion that a product is animal-free cannot resolve questions about every earlier production stage.

Public policy concerns permission to sell, research support, standards, disclosure and the institutions responsible for those decisions. The plausible connection to consumption is through products, prices, access or trust. The current documentary evidence records instruments and events; it does not identify their effects on purchases. A funded project remains distinct from a commercial product reaching a household.

The proposed sequence from acceptability to trial, purchase and habit is an analytical framework. It is not an estimated causal model. Trial could replace meat, replace a familiar plant dish or add an occasional novelty. Repeated purchases and the displaced food would need to be observed before the study could assess substitution.

## Pakistan has more poultry availability while India has more pulses

FAOSTAT records {{claim:fbs_2023_pakistan_poultry}} kg of poultry available per person per year in Pakistan and {{claim:fbs_2023_india_poultry}} in India in {{year:2023}}. For pulses, the order reverses: {{claim:fbs_2023_india_pulses}} kg in India and {{claim:fbs_2023_pakistan_pulses}} in Pakistan. Milk excluding butter is {{claim:fbs_2023_india_milk}} kg in India and {{claim:fbs_2023_pakistan_milk}} in Pakistan. The source is the saved FAOSTAT food-balance release, with its flags and commodity definitions retained. {{cite:fao_fbs_2025_10_28|FAOSTAT food balances}}

Food balance sheets estimate food available for human consumption within a national accounting system. They do not measure an individual's ingestion, household distribution or retail sales of alternative proteins. The series can compare recorded availability under the source's definitions. It cannot identify who consumed the food, how much was wasted after supply entered households or which products competed at a particular meal.

The pulse comparison matters because familiar plant foods are already part of ordinary food systems. Large pulse availability may be compatible with interest in a meat analogue, indifference to it or preference for a familiar dish. None of those responses follows from supply alone. A study that compares an analogue only with meat would miss a relevant existing option for some consumers.

The OECD–FAO Agricultural Outlook uses a separate method and weight convention. Its poultry projection is shown apart from the historical food-balance series, with its vintage and retail-weight unit. Joining the projected value directly to the FAOSTAT endpoint would create an apparent change partly from incompatible definitions. The report treats the outlook as a conditional projection from its source rather than an extension of measured household consumption. {{cite:oecd_fao_outlook_2025_2034|OECD–FAO Agricultural Outlook}}

For {{year:2034}}, that outlook projects poultry consumption at {{claim:oecd_2034_india_poultry_meat_oecd_human_consumption_projection}} kg per person per year in India and {{claim:oecd_2034_pakistan_poultry_meat_oecd_human_consumption_projection}} in Pakistan, in retail weight. The source release covers {{year:2025–2034}}. The numerical difference from the food-balance endpoint is not an estimated decline under a common measurement system.

## Regional food frequency and household quantities answer different questions

India's National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5) measures reported food frequency among specified respondent groups. Its fish, chicken or meat category combines foods and records frequency rather than portion size. The regional panels retain the sex, age group, residence, fieldwork period and tabulation base applicable to each row. A weekly-frequency percentage cannot be converted to kilograms or grams of protein without information the table does not provide. {{cite:nfhs5_india_2019_21|National Family Health Survey}}

Pakistan's Household Integrated Economic Survey, HIES, supplies household-based quantities and expenditure measures. The revised quantity workbook reports monthly per-capita quantities, so calling them household totals would change their meaning. Its regional chicken rows can describe differences within that survey's design. They cannot be placed on the same colour scale as India's reported weekly food frequency and interpreted as a bilateral dietary ranking. {{cite:pakistan_hies_2024_25_table23|HIES revised quantity table}}

The Pakistan National Nutrition Survey contributes separate nutrition context. Its population and indicators differ from HIES food quantities. The maps and tables retain geographic coverage gaps and distinguish the former merged areas where the source requires it. A missing join to the chosen boundary file is an unresolved mapping problem, not a zero observation.

Residence differences in these tables are descriptive. Urban and rural respondents or households differ in many measured and unmeasured circumstances. Neither the map nor the residence toggle identifies an effect of urbanisation. The useful follow-up is to examine access, prices, expenditure and meal practices in specified locations before selecting study sites.

## Food budgets and protein shares show different dietary structures

Pakistan's chicken quantity rises across the selected expenditure quintiles in the revised HIES table. This is a per-capita quantity gradient within one source, not a measured preference for an alternative product. Household size also differs across these groups, which makes denominator discipline necessary. A household total and a per-capita quantity can order groups differently without either describing the same estimand.

The lowest quintile records {{claim:hies_chicken_meat_q1}} kg of chicken per person per month, compared with {{claim:hies_chicken_meat_q5}} in the highest. The accompanying household-size context falls from {{claim:hies_household_size_q1}} to {{claim:hies_household_size_q5}} persons across those groups. The quantities come from the revised HIES {{year:2024–25}} workbook, with the precise cells recorded in the claims ledger. The exact analytical base for that revised table is not established by the report's broader household-count context.

India's Household Consumption Expenditure Survey, HCES, converts reported household food into nutrient estimates and reports protein contributions by food group. Cereals, pulses, dairy and the combined eggs, fish and meat group each have a distinct role. The combined animal-food group in that table excludes dairy. Its share cannot be described as all animal-source protein. {{cite:mospi_nutrition_report_594_2025|Nutritional Intake in India}}

The Indian fractile labels rank the population by monthly per-capita consumption expenditure (MPCE), from lower to higher spending. Rural and urban populations are ranked separately. The labels describe positions in those expenditure distributions; they are not percentages below a protein requirement or shares of the food budget.

These tables broaden the comparative question beyond replacing meat. A product might compete with a cereal accompaniment, a pulse dish, dairy or a convenience meal. The report cannot determine that competition from ingredient composition. It requires asking consumers about a specified occasion and recording the food actually replaced.

Expenditure measures need equal care. A food-budget share is a proportion of food expenditure, not total household expenditure, energy or protein. A quantity gradient is not a price elasticity. The source rows retain those distinctions so that apparent agreement between indicators does not create an unsupported common scale.

## The Indian benchmark is a scenario and the Pakistan total is missing

India's published consumer-unit estimates are the appropriate denominator for the report's adult reference scenarios. A consumer unit expresses energy needs relative to a sedentary adult man. It is not a protein-equivalent person. Comparing a household group's mean protein estimate with an adult requirement therefore supplies a descriptive scenario, with an explicit mismatch that prevents an adequacy conclusion.

The all-India estimates are {{claim:india_cu_rural_mpce_all_india}} g per consumer unit per day in rural areas and {{claim:india_cu_urban_mpce_all_india}} in urban areas. HCES fieldwork ran from August {{year:2023}} through July {{year:2024}}; these months describe the survey period, not an individual respondent's observation interval. The nutrient table's analytical base remains distinct from the survey's overall household sample.

The Estimated Average Requirement, EAR, is the primary reference; the Recommended Dietary Allowance, RDA, is retained as a labelled sensitivity. They answer different questions about requirements and cannot be swapped merely to produce a larger apparent gap. The report also retains alternative body-weight assumptions. None of these comparisons observes the distribution of usual individual intake needed for an estimate of population inadequacy.

At a reference weight of {{claim:requirement_india_ear_60kg_weight}} kg, the EAR scenario uses {{claim:requirement_india_ear_60kg_coefficient}} g per kg per day, or {{claim:requirement_india_ear_60kg}} g per day. The RDA sensitivity uses {{claim:requirement_india_rda_60kg_coefficient}} g per kg per day, or {{claim:requirement_india_rda_60kg}} g per day. These calculations are tagged derived even though their requirement coefficients come from reference sources. They are adult scenarios without a protein-equivalent household denominator.

A group mean above a reference can coexist with individuals below it. A group mean below a reference does not tell how many individuals are below it. Counting expenditure groups is especially misleading when those groups differ in population or household composition. The former unweighted fraction of groups below a benchmark has therefore been removed rather than described as a population deficiency rate.

Pakistan's former full-diet estimate used a pooled-basket cost calibration that did not establish complete protein intake. The current model withdraws that total and preserves it only as historical output. The replacement converts selected reported food quantities through named food-composition factors and edible-portion assumptions. It reports a matched-food subtotal and a separate subtotal for chicken, beef, mutton, eggs and fish.

The composition evidence comes from the {{cite:pakistan_food_composition_2001|Pakistan Food Composition Table}}, {{cite:bangladesh_fct_2013|Food Composition Table for Bangladesh}}, {{cite:india_ifct_2017|Indian Food Composition Tables}} and {{cite:usda_fdc_sr_legacy_2018|USDA SR Legacy release}}. Pakistan-table food entries anchor the local matches; the Bangladesh Rohu entry supplies distinct edible-portion assumptions, while Indian and USDA entries supply explicitly named food-form alternatives. The {{cite:fao_infoods_density_v2_2012|INFOODS density database}} supports volume-to-mass assumptions. {{cite:usda_sr_legacy_documentation_2018|USDA documentation}} defines the edible-portion basis; its portion weights do not establish Pakistani egg-size frequencies. The model also treats tea-transfer and product-mixture choices as conditional assumptions. These sources provide composition and method inputs, not measured Pakistani intake.

For each named endpoint, the [scenario-factor table](../methods/factor_evidence.csv) identifies the conditional food conversions. Its factor identifiers join the [source-factor catalogue](../methods/source_factor_catalog.csv), which gives original PDF pages, food codes, units and source-file hashes. The claims ledger preserves that join and the complete composition-source list alongside the household quantity cells. The [method guide](../methods/README.md) explains how to follow this record without treating an assumed food match as an observed product mix.

The national matched-food subtotal is {{claim:pakistan_partial_all_all_matched_basket_subtotal_low}} to {{claim:pakistan_partial_all_all_matched_basket_subtotal_high}} g per person per day. The separate chicken, beef, mutton, eggs and fish subtotal is {{claim:pakistan_partial_all_all_five_animal_foods_subtotal_low}} to {{claim:pakistan_partial_all_all_five_animal_foods_subtotal_high}} g per person per day. The low method is `bd_cuts_rohu_us_small`; the high method is `edible_flesh_us_jumbo`. Their differences concern edible portions, fish matching and egg-size assumptions, rather than sampled uncertainty.

Of {{claim:pakistan_model_n_table23_foods}} food rows, {{claim:pakistan_model_n_converted_foods}} receive a conditional conversion; {{claim:pakistan_model_imputed_food_count}} are imputed. The separate animal-food subtotal excludes dairy and other animal-source foods. Neither subtotal is called total protein intake, and no quintile ordering is inferred from these incomplete sums.

The method range expresses the extrema among named conditional conversions. It is not a sampling confidence interval or a complete uncertainty bound. Unresolved foods, prepared-food coverage, food-form mixtures and quantity reconciliation remain outside the subtotal. No imputation is used to silently complete the diet. The range therefore remains separate from the Indian consumer-unit panel and from the adult EAR/RDA scenarios.

The unresolved food quantities and local product mixtures are the next inputs needed before a complete Pakistani intake estimate can be attempted.

## Affordability and child growth reveal overlapping concerns

SOFI estimates that {{claim:nutrition_2024_india_unaffordable}} percent of India's population and {{claim:nutrition_2024_pakistan_unaffordable}} percent of Pakistan's could not afford a healthy diet in {{year:2024}}. The measure combines an economic-access model with a specified healthy-diet cost. It does not observe every household's actual food purchases or diagnose a nutrient deficiency. The selected source vintage remains attached to the figures. {{cite:fao_sofi_2025|The State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World}}

The JME country model estimates under-five stunting at {{claim:nutrition_2024_india_stunting}} percent and {{claim:nutrition_2024_pakistan_stunting}} percent respectively for {{year:2024}}. The country-model release and survey observations appear separately because a modelled annual estimate is not a new household measurement. The model rows retain uncertainty intervals and provenance where supplied. {{cite:jme_country_model_march2026|Joint Child Malnutrition Estimates country model}}

Stunting reflects accumulated growth constraints with multiple causes. It cannot be used as a protein-deficiency measure or as an addressable market for a meat substitute. Undernourishment likewise uses its own definition, population and estimation procedure. The same person can fall within several indicators, and their reference periods can differ. Adding their counts would double-count people and combine incompatible outcomes.

The report therefore presents an explicit instruction not to add these indicators. Their joint relevance is to research design: affordability, dietary quality and child growth deserve attention, but each needs an appropriate intervention question. The current evidence does not establish that a particular alternative-protein product is affordable, suitable for the affected population or effective against any of these outcomes.

## Exploratory supply forecasts need better calibration evidence

::: {data-package="P08"}

The standalone benchmark models annual national protein supply using frozen historical series. Total and animal-source protein are separate series for each country. Its measured outcome is available supply, not alternative-protein purchases, consumption frequency or an adoption share. The selected forecast horizon extends to {{year:2035}}, subject to the benchmark's assumptions and short observed history.

The frozen input contains {{claim:package_p08_annual_observations}} annual observations and the selected output contains {{claim:package_p08_selected_forecasts}} forecast records. The protocol defaults to persistence because its model-selection evidence is too short to support a more ambitious selection claim. Consequently, the selected point forecasts retain the last observed level while uncertainty grows with the horizon. A flat point path is a stated benchmark assumption, not evidence that supply will be constant.

{{benchmark_table}}

At the nominal {{claim:benchmark_india_total_nominal_level}} percent interval level, the selected total-supply interval covers {{claim:benchmark_india_total_coverage_count}} of {{claim:benchmark_india_total_evaluation_count}} held-out Indian cells and {{claim:benchmark_pakistan_total_coverage_count}} of {{claim:benchmark_pakistan_total_evaluation_count}} Pakistani cells. The animal-source series has the same country-specific coverage counts: {{claim:benchmark_india_animal_coverage_count}} of {{claim:benchmark_india_animal_evaluation_count}} for India and {{claim:benchmark_pakistan_animal_coverage_count}} of {{claim:benchmark_pakistan_animal_evaluation_count}} for Pakistan. The evaluation rows and split remain attached to these results.

The held-out comparison reports very different interval coverage across countries. The report retains the actual counts and evaluation denominator alongside the nominal interval level. A small set of overlapping evaluation cells cannot demonstrate stable calibration. Broad intervals may cover observations without producing useful precision, while narrow intervals can miss them repeatedly. Neither result supports interpreting the forecast as a calibrated statement about future consumers.

The uneven held-out coverage is a reason to investigate the interval method before using these supply scenarios for decisions. Neither the calibration exercise nor its forecast contains a measured alternative-protein market share.

The missing adoption baseline cannot be supplied by relabelling this model. A future adoption study would need observations of the relevant product and outcome, a compatible denominator and a resolution source. Until those inputs exist, the supply benchmark remains a separate research product.

The package also contains statistical diagnostics, a hypothesised causal graph and a policy calculator with assumed pilot budgets. These describe proposed relationships or analyst-selected scenarios. Installed causal-analysis software does not identify an effect, and a calculator's arithmetic does not validate its national parameters. The evidence index supports source retrieval without generating a new consumer observation.

## Industry records establish events rather than a market total

The company register distinguishes a business, a product category, a dated event and its evidence. A directory listing is not proof that a factory operates at its stated capacity. A company website can document a claim or offer without independently confirming sales. Closures and exclusions remain visible so that an earlier optimistic record is not silently carried forward as current operation.

UnCrave's reported closure is recorded as a dated event. The announcement date does not establish the exact date on which every product ceased to be available. The Phyx company lead is recorded as an India lead rather than a Pakistan company. The food/feed rule excludes feed-only activity from human-food company totals and retains adjacent or mixed-use businesses with explicit category limits. A traditional soy-food or plant-milk business does not become a meat-alternative producer merely because its inputs are plant-derived.

Pakistan's identified retail material is similarly bounded. A catalogue or import-to-order offer demonstrates that a seller presented a product under particular terms. It does not prove current stock, completed sales, nationwide availability or repeat purchasing. Dated secondary reporting on a business remains reporting from that date unless a newer operational source is recovered.

Pulse production provides potential feedstock context, with crop definitions and source flags retained. Whole-crop tonnage can have several end uses and cannot be converted into alternative-meat output, isolate capacity or sales without additional process and allocation evidence. Aggregates and constituent crops also overlap, so they are not added as independent supplies.

The bounded public-source search recovered newer industry, consumer-awareness, export and global-market material. It did not establish a compatible newer estimate of India's domestic alternative-protein market. This is a narrower conclusion than saying no newer GFI information exists. Historical national scenarios, global sales and export opportunities remain different quantities.

## A speculative proxy estimate places both markets far below the US per person

The project team agreed to answer one question three ways: how large the alternative-protein market will be in {{year:2026}}, {{year:2030}} and {{year:2035}}. ## Halal access depends on the named product and decision

::: {data-package="P09"}

The cultivated-chicken paper asks what must be established before a specified product reaches Muslim consumers through the relevant religious, certification and food-authorization processes. Its framework separates donor procurement, culture inputs, transformation, category and edibility, certification, and food authorization. These are analytical questions for organizing the evidence, not a claim that every authority reasons in the same sequence. [Cultivated Chicken and Halal Market Access](https://cultivated-chicken-halal.vercel.app/)

The International Islamic Fiqh Academy's {{year:2025}} resolution makes its cultivated-meat position conditional on the donor, culture inputs and oversight. It excludes prohibited culture media and ingredients, requires supervision by a credible specialist authority, and assigns disclosure and verification duties. Its donor wording distinguishes a living lawful animal from cases requiring lawful slaughter; that wording needs interpretation within the full resolution. {{cite:IIFA265|IIFA cultivated-meat resolution}}. These conditions do not constitute certification of a particular manufacturer's product.

Singapore's Islamic Religious Council (MUIS) stated in {{year:2024}} that the cell source must be a halal animal, ingredients contributing to texture and composition must be halal, and the product must be clean and non-toxic. {{cite:MUIS2024|MUIS cultivated-meat guidance}}. That guidance concerns cultivated meat under Singapore's religious framework. It does not grant Pakistani food authorization or determine consumer willingness.

A founder cell line means cells selected and banked to start later production. The original animal sample, that banked line and the later biomass can raise different questions. A serum-free production entry does not by itself document all earlier banking stages. Washing, measured residues, biological growth and legal transformation are distinct claims. The paper leaves the evidential connections between those stages open where a product dossier is missing.

The source audit distinguishes admitted evidence, held material and sources not retrieved. Every category retains its human-review status. Classical passages, modern institutional decisions, applicant dossiers and academic interpretations have different authority and scope. An English abstract does not support a reconstruction of an unread full text, and a regulator's hosting of an applicant dossier does not make it a religious ruling.

The paper register contains {{claim:package_p09_source_records}} records: {{claim:package_p09_sources_verified_with_note}} verified with a note by the recorded checking process, {{claim:package_p09_sources_held}} held and {{claim:package_p09_sources_not_retrieved}} not retrieved. All {{claim:package_p09_human_verification_pending}} retain human verification pending. ## Cultural context describes variation without predicting demand

The culture matrix records dimensions such as dietary identity, religion and food practice, geography, household context and relevant institutions. Each entry retains its source and date. These observations help design questions and identify populations omitted by a proposed sample. They do not yield a national willingness score.

Vegetarian self-identification differs from reported frequency of eating fish, chicken or meat. A respondent's definition can differ from a researcher's category, while frequency alone does not reveal quantities or motives. The report does not turn either measure into a market segment for a manufactured analogue. A person already avoiding meat has familiar alternatives that need to be included in the choice set.

Pakistan's urban share and halal institutions also describe different dimensions. Population geography does not measure exposure to alternative proteins, and an institutional mandate does not measure personal trust. Relevant follow-up questions would record actual shopping access, information exposure, household decision-making and product-specific assurances rather than infer them from residence or affiliation.

The strongest interpretation supported here is that a uniform national consumer does not describe the evidence. Whether particular social or religious conditions independently affect purchase remains a question for a comparable study with specified products and prices.

## The next data collection should answer a named question

The first task is to establish whether the identified respondent-data routes contain reusable observations with compatible product and outcome definitions. Before analysis, each acquisition should record the file version, licence, sampling frame, language, recruitment, fieldwork dates, exclusions and exact variable meanings. A public repository title is insufficient evidence for any of these details.

A product-availability study would separately define locations, retailers, observation dates and what counts as available. An import-to-order listing should remain distinct from an item physically in stock. Recording package size and price per relevant serving would support an affordability comparison, while a stock record alone would not establish sales.

A small consumer pilot could then test the meal-based comparison proposed in the brief. It should include people with different food expenditures, dietary practices and responsibilities for shopping or cooking. The immediate outcome would be whether the product description and choice task are understood. A purposive pilot would not estimate national demand.

Repeated purchasing and displacement require follow-up observations. A diary or transaction study should record whether the new product replaced meat, dairy, pulses or another food, and whether the change persisted. Those outcomes would make the later forecast question more concrete. Until such evidence exists, the appropriate contribution is a traceable account of current conditions and explicit unanswered questions.
