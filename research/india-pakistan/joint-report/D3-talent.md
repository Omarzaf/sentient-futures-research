# D3. Talent and resource allocation

> AI-assisted working draft, 17 October 2026. Human verification pending.

Fund employer-linked technical trials in India and a small ingredient-application pilot in Pakistan before expanding training. This is an allocation proposal, not an estimated ranking of returns. AltProtein.Jobs vacancies are demand signals on one job board. They do not measure employment, successful hiring or skill shortages.

**Which skills does India's job market already show?** The larger vacancy series supports asking employers about technical hiring. It does not establish available skills. The workbook has country totals and growth columns, but no role, function or vacancy category column. Directory labels include plant-based and cultivated ingredients. These classify businesses, not advertised jobs. Product formulation, food safety and bioprocess operations are therefore questions for employers, not measured occupational findings.

**What does Pakistan's base support?** Its directory entries name Shafi Gluco Chem and Shafi Gluco Chem (Pvt) Limited. Treat these as potentially overlapping records. Both rows record 0 postings since October 2024; the end date is unrecorded. Their plant-based ingredient labels suggest candidate partners for application testing, not verified fermentation or cultivated-meat capacity. An initial grant could support ingredient characterization, food-safety documentation and a local formulation trial. Confirm facilities, staff and production status before funding equipment.

**Where would funding change capacity fastest?** Neither altprotein_jobs.csv, its vacancy workbook nor research/ai-protein/literature-review.md measures time to hire or time to build capacity. Test it through short grants with baseline and completion checks. In India, ask employers to nominate a specific technical constraint and compare training with access to shared facilities. In Pakistan, test whether an ingredient pilot can produce reproducible specifications and a usable formulation. Compare time to validated results before expanding either programme.

The September literature review makes computational-talent funding conditional on evidence that computation, data or experimental design limits progress. It cannot automatically replace bioprocess engineers, operators or regulatory scientists. Pair any computational award with experimental validation. A laboratory improvement alone does not establish lower commercial prices or market growth. Use these trials to test forward talent assumptions, without adding vacancies as another market-growth multiplier.
