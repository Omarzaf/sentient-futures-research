# D7. Three studies to test household demand

These proposed studies develop [the demand hypotheses in REPORT.md](../report/REPORT.md#the-drivers-are-hypotheses-about-decisions). They require new data and separate approval. None is an observed India or Pakistan result.

1. **Entry cost.** Does a smaller pack increase first purchase more than a lower unit price? Randomize pack size and unit price independently, holding product and availability constant. Record full-pack cash outlay, price per meal, household budget and actual first purchase. Evidence against the entry-cost mechanism would be no meaningful pack-outlay effect once unit price is controlled, with uncertainty narrow enough to exclude a useful effect.

2. **Household meal fit.** Does fitting a shared family meal increase repeat purchase after incentives end, compared with an individual trial? Randomize meal-use guidance while holding product, price and access constant. Collect meal diaries, household participation, taste ratings and dated purchase receipts. Evidence against the mechanism would be similar unsubsidized repeat purchase across groups despite a verified difference in shared-meal use.

3. **Replacement.** Does the second purchase replace chicken, pulses or nothing? Follow household purchases before trial and after incentives end, including non-triers as a comparison. Record quantities, prices, household size and all competing foods. Evidence against animal-food displacement would be unchanged animal-food purchases alongside repeat alternative-protein purchases, or replacement of pulses alone.

The 16 October 2026 retail capture, baseline-completion-20261016/final/source-tables/retail/coverage.json, contains 97 verified prices from one outlet per country: Modern Bazaar in India and Naheed.pk in Pakistan. It has 0 verified protein values and no plant-based meat category. Expand outlets and add that category before comparing prices; verify protein content before any protein-cost comparison.
