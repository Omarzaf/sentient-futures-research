# Protein Consumption and Alternative Protein Adoption in India and Pakistan

[Read the brief](india-pakistan-brief.md) or [open the formatted report](index.html). Evidence reviewed through 14 September 2026. Current research scope; AI-assisted draft with human verification pending.

This is the consolidated secondary synthesis for the current India–Pakistan direction described in [CURRENT-SCOPE.md](../../CURRENT-SCOPE.md). It examines how affordability, dietary practice, social legitimacy, certification, and public policy shape whether a substitute product earns a recurring place in a household meal, and it proposes the comparative tests and forecast-question requirements that would follow.

- [Evidence register](source-register.json): 20 records from this review, with page locators, access notes, findings, limitations, and funding or conflict statements. Some records are supplementary and are not cited in the brief.

## What this document establishes and does not

The brief is a review of published evidence. It contains no interviews, tasting trials, consumer survey, or forecasting experiment conducted for this project. National statistics from the two countries are read within their own survey designs and are deliberately not placed on a single intake scale. Consumer-study percentages are bounded to their samples and are not converted into adoption rates or market sizes.

The policy section reports a documented Indian smart-protein programme and documented Pakistani halal institutions. It does not establish that Pakistan lacks relevant policy; that claim would require an institutional, date-bounded search the review did not perform.
